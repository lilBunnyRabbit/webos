## Drive Hub

Driving games along one street. The app opens on the gate and the first buildings, your car at the gate; the view follows the car once you drive, and the street runs on east with a building for every game.

- Each building carries its game's name and icon on the roof. Drive into its lit doorway and press **Enter** (or tap **Enter** on the card) to play. The building you played last has a glowing doorway. The car park holds **Tight Spot**, the **Warehouse** the forklift game, the ferry deck **Ferry Loader**.
- **Games** at the left of the toolbar jumps straight to the yard or any game, and marks the one you played last.
- **Back** in a game returns you to the yard, out of that game's doorway.

## The yard is a playground

- Nothing is judged here. Touching something just stops you with a bump; back away and carry on.
- Stop on the **Garage** pad to pick any of 41 vehicles, shelf by shelf: **Cars** (hatchback to camper van and a stretch limousine), **Service** (taxi, police car, ambulance, fire engine, ladder truck, garbage truck, baggage train), **Buses** (city bus, double-decker, coach, articulated bus), **Trucks** (box truck, semi-trailer truck, truck and drawbar trailer, road train), **Towing** (trailer, caravan, boat, horse box, tractor, tractor and two trailers, quad), **Machines** (forklift, skid-steer loader, telehandler, wheel loader, articulated dump truck, zero-turn mower, tank) and **Fun** (tuk-tuk, golf cart, ice resurfacer, shopping trolley, F1 car). It waits on the pad, ready to drive off, and stays yours in the yard until you close the window. The police car, ambulance and fire engine flash their lights while in gear.
- The garage pad, the cones (a slalom) and the bays along the north wall (practice parking) are by the gate.
- The gears, pedals, wheel, sensors and settings work as in every game below.

## Tight Spot: the goal

Park the car in the marked bay without touching anything. The bay pulses; its arrow shows which way the nose must point (two heads: either way).

## The car

- It steers like a real car: it turns about its rear wheels, so the front swings wide and the back cuts in. The front wheels show where they point.
- Full lock gives a 5.3 m turning radius. The wheel turns at a hand's speed, and it can turn while the car stands still.
- In **D** or **R** the car moves only while you press the gas, and rolls to a stop when you let go. Turn on **Creep** in settings to have it roll on its own, like a real automatic.
- The dashed lines show where the leading corners will go at the current wheel angle.

## Vehicles

- Each spot hands you a vehicle, named on the start card. Car spots take the cars, the service cars, the tuk-tuk, the golf cart, the skid-steer loader and the mower, and most take the tank and the telehandler (some leave out the limousine, the camper van, the tank or the telehandler where they don't fit); the bus stop takes the city bus, the double-decker, the articulated bus or the wheel loader; the depot those, the coach, the garbage truck, the fire engine, the dump truck and the ladder truck (the articulated bus drives through its depot nose first); the driveway a trailer, a horse box or the quad; the slipway a trailer or the boat; the camping pitch the caravan; the loading dock the semi-trailer truck, the box truck, the drawbar truck, the road train, the wheel loader, the dump truck or the ladder truck (the box truck also takes the bus stop and the depot). Bigger ones need wider swings, and the bays and gaps grow to fit them. Levels keep the vehicle they always had.
- Rather pick? In **Spots** with a kind chosen, and in the **Open lot**, the **Vehicle** picker next to the spot picker takes Any vehicle or one the spot allows (on a narrow window it sits on the start card). The open lot starts in the hatchback and also takes the van, the pickup, the car and trailer, the F1 car, the tuk-tuk or the golf cart. Levels and the daily keep their own vehicle.
- The **F1 car** is fast and stops hard, but its wheels barely turn: it needs as much room to swing as a lorry.
- The **bus** has a 12 m body and a long tail behind the rear wheels: when it turns, the tail swings out the other way.
- The **trailer** pivots on the tow ball. Towing forward it follows and straightens. Reversing it goes the opposite way to the wheel: steer left to send it right, and catch it early. Past about 70° it jackknifes and the round ends. The guide lines follow the trailer's tail while you reverse, and the rear sensors read from it.
- The **articulated bus** bends in the middle: its back half follows like a trailer, so it folds the same way when you reverse. The **coach** has a tag axle behind the drive axle; it turns about the middle of the two.
- **Trains of trailers** (the drawbar truck's dolly and body, the road train, the baggage train, the tractor with two trailers) bend at every hinge. Forward they all fall in line behind you; reversing, each hinge swings the next one further, so a long one can't be backed far. Every hinge has its own limit (a semi's kingpin about 80°, a drawbar 60°, a drawbar trailer's turntable 45°, a baggage cart 75°), and folding any of them past it ends the round: the card says which hinge.
- The **loading dock**: the semi starts set up on the apron, its trailer's tail at the free door. Back it in square, steering the other way to swing the trailer, and stop short of the dock bumpers. The drawbar truck and the road train can't be backed in: their dock has lanes you drive straight into, nose first.
- **Machines that steer differently.** The start card and the garage card say how each one drives.
  - **Skid steer** (the skid-steer loader, the mower, the tank): the wheel slows one side. Half lock turns about the inner side; full lock spins it on the spot as soon as you give it gas. The tank's tracks run against each other as it turns.
  - **Frame steer** (the wheel loader, the dump truck): the wheel bends it in the middle, even standing still, and the back follows the front. Both halves have to be straight in the bay.
  - **All-wheel steer** (the ladder truck): the back wheels turn the other way, so it turns in half the room and its tail swings out the other side.
  - **Crab steer** (the telehandler): steers all four wheels like the ladder truck; stop and press **X** or tap **Crab** and every wheel turns the same way, so it slides sideways without turning. Press it again to steer normally.
- The **camping pitch**: you start lined up on the track, the car swung out and the caravan's tail at the pitch. Back it in caravan first; steer the other way to swing it, and keep it off the hedges.

## Keyboard

- **← →** or **A D** turn the wheel: a tap nudges it, and held a moment it turns faster. **↑** or **W** drives forward, **↓** or **S** backs up: against the way you're going it brakes, and pressing it again once the car stands switches to D or R.
- **E** drive, **R** or **Q** reverse, **N** neutral, **P** or **Space** park, or click a gear at the top left. **C** centres the wheel. **X** switches the telehandler's crab mode (stopped).
- **+** and **-** zoom. **Escape** pauses and resumes.
- **Enter** starts, resumes, and once the round is over takes the card's main button: a new spot, the next level, or another try after a touch. **R** tries the same spot again.
- The keys keep driving after you click a button or close the settings.
- Settings choose whether the wheel centres itself when you let go (it does as the car rolls, like a real one) or stays where you left it.

## Touch

- Drag the **steering wheel** round by its rim; it stays where you leave it. Tap the middle to centre it.
- Rather tap? Set **Steering** to **Buttons** in settings and hold **◀** or **▶**; like the keys, they wind the wheel back faster when you hold them against where it points. Settings also choose whether the wheel centres itself when you let go.
- **Gas** drives forward and **Brake** backs up, like the arrow keys: against the way you're going it brakes, and pressing it again once the car stands switches to D or R. Brake in P reverses.
- Tap **P**, **R**, **N** or **D** in the strip at the top to pick a gear; P parks. On the telehandler **Crab** next to it switches crab mode when stopped.
- Held sideways, the map fills the screen and the wheel and pedals sit over its sides; the time and moves move up next to the gears.
- Pinch the map to zoom. **Back** pauses a round, then takes you to the yard.

## Tight Spot judging

- Touching a car, wall, kerb, pillar, post, hedge or the water ends the round, like a driving test. The red ring shows where.
- It counts as parked when the whole car (and its trailer) is inside the bay lines, within 5° of the bay, stopped and in **P**.
- **Moves** are changes between D and R. Stars come from moves: at the spot's par or under is three, up to two more is two. The start card shows the par.
- **Precision** says how neatly you parked: centred between the side lines (↔), centred end to end (↕) and square to the bay (∠). Dead centre and straight is 100%; touching a line or 5° off is nothing. With a trailer the car and the trailer count together. 95% or more is **Perfect**. Precision doesn't change the stars.
- Bests are kept per spot and vehicle: fewest moves, then the quickest, plus the best precision you've reached there.
- In **P** outside a bay the line at the bottom says what's off: not inside a bay, not straight, or facing the wrong way.
- Parking sensors watch the bumper you're driving towards: the beeps quicken as it closes in on something and go steady under 30 cm. The garage is where you'll lean on them.
- The **end bay** sits in the corner of a full, narrow aisle with no room past it: get in with a few short moves, back and forth. Par allows two.

## Tight Spot modes

- **Spots:** a random spot of the kind you pick, or any. The daily is the same spot for everyone, and Game Hub keeps your results.
- **Levels:** 48 spots in three laps, each tighter and busier: the nine car spots, then the bus stop, the end bay, the driveway, the depot, the slipway, the camping pitch and the loading dock. Park one to open the next.
- **Open lot:** a block of streets and car parks; park in any free bay.

## Warehouse: the forklift

Move pallets with a forklift. A job is one to four pallets, each to be taken from its marked slot to its marked target, in order: a rack bay (**B4**) or a spot at the dock (**Dock C**). The start card lists them and shows the whole floor; the pallet in hand pulses with its number on it, its target is dashed with an arrow and its number, and an arrow on the edge of the view points the way when it's off screen. The chip at the top says what's next: *Pallet 2/3 · pick up at B4*, then *Pallet 2/3 → Dock C*.

- **It steers at the back.** The rear wheels turn, so the forklift pivots near its front wheels and the counterweight swings out wide: watch the tail when you turn beside a rack. The dashed lines show where the corners will go.
- **Lift:** drive the forks straight into the pallet until they're fully in (the chip shows *Forks 100%*), stop, and press **F** or **Space**, or tap **Lift**. Both tines have to go under the pallet: square to it within 5° and no more than 7 cm off its centre, or they jam. Standing short of it, the hint says how far off you are.
- **Lower:** carry the pallet into its target, straight, stop, and press **F** again (**Lower**). The button is off until it would work; stopped near a pallet or a target, a line at the bottom says what's off.
- **Contact ends the job:** the forklift, its forks or the pallet on them touching a rack, a pillar, a wall or another pallet. Forks pushed into a pallet crooked or off its middle jam.
- The forklift backs up with a beeper and its amber beacon turns while it works. It tops out at about 9 km/h.

## Warehouse scoring

- Three stars: one for finishing the job, one for the moves at or under par, one for the time at or under the par time. The par time comes from the computer driving the job itself, at a careful pace with room to spare; it appears on the card a moment after the job does.
- **Moves** are changes between D and R, as in Tight Spot. Each pallet needs four drives at least (in, back out, in, back out), so par is five per pallet less two.
- **Precision** scores each pallet you put down, centred and square in its target, like a park in Tight Spot; the card shows the average and every drop. 95% or more is **Perfect**.
- A best is more stars, then a quicker time; it's kept per level, per job size and per daily.

## Warehouse modes

- **Jobs:** a random warehouse and job of the size you pick, one to four pallets. **New** deals another.
- **Levels:** 16 jobs, four of each size, the aisles and bays tighter as you go. Finish one to open the next.
- **Daily:** the same three-pallet job for everyone today. Game Hub lists it as Drive Hub · Forklift, next to Tight Spot's daily.

## Hot Lap: the race track

Three laps in the F1 car on one of three circuits: **Meadow** (gentle), **Harbour** (a hairpin and a chicane) and **Ridge** (fast and flowing). Pick one at the top. The start card shows the whole track, its length, the par time and your best.

- **The lap.** You start on the grid just behind the chequered line. Crossing it starts lap 1; the clock stops each time you cross it again. The dashed lines across the track split each lap into three sectors.
- **Grip.** The faster you go, the less the wheels turn: brake before a bend, turn in, and accelerate out. The dashed racing line (shown with **Guides** in settings) is the quickest way round.
- **Grass slows you** right down, but the lap still counts. The red and white kerbs are part of the track.
- **A wall voids the lap.** The car stops dead where it touched; back away and carry on. The lap shows **Void** and won't count, but the next one starts clean at the line.
- **Ghost.** Your best lap on each track is saved and drives with you, faint, on every lap after. Passing a sector line shows how far ahead (green) or behind you are against it.
- **Stars** for your best clean lap: one for a clean lap, two within 20% of par, three within 8%. Par is the computer's lap on the racing line at the car's limit, so it's tight.
- **Daily:** the track of the day for everyone, ranked by your quickest clean lap. Game Hub lists it as Drive Hub · F1.

## Ferry Loader

Load a ferry. A queue of three to six vehicles waits behind the quay: cars, vans, box trucks, now and then a bus. Each one in turn comes onto the quay; drive it up the ramp at the stern and park it in any lane on the deck. The start card lists the queue, and the chip at the top says which one you're driving and what's next.

- **Stow it:** straight in its lane, centred (within 25 cm of the lane's middle), its nose 0.3 to 1 m behind the vehicle ahead, or the bow if the lane is empty. Then shift into **P** (or press **Space** or **Enter**). The window to stop in is painted across each lane; the lane you're in lights up. Not quite there, the line at the bottom says what's off: *Pull up: 1.8 m to the bow*, *Off centre: 32 cm to port*, *Across two lanes*.
- **The next one** comes onto the quay as soon as one is stowed. It starts lined up with the middle of the ramp, so on a four-lane deck you steer into a lane.
- **Undo** (**U**, or the **Undo** button) takes the last vehicle off again and back to the quay, to try another lane. The clock keeps running.
- **Balance:** the gauge at the top shows how the load leans, to port (left, facing the bow) or starboard. A bus weighs as much as twelve city cars. Stay inside the green band.
- **Contact ends the sailing:** the ship's side, the bow, the water by the ramp, the quay fence or a vehicle already aboard.

## Ferry Loader scoring

- Three stars: one for getting every vehicle aboard, one for the balance within 12 % at the end, one for the time at or under the par time. The par comes from the computer loading the same queue at a careful pace; it appears on the card a moment after the sailing does.
- A best is more stars, then a quicker time; it's kept per level, per number of vehicles and per daily.

## Ferry Loader modes

- **Sailings:** a random ferry and queue, three to six vehicles. **New** deals another.
- **Levels:** 12 sailings, three each of three to six vehicles, the lanes and the stern tighter as you go. Finish one to open the next.
- **Daily:** the same five-vehicle queue for everyone today. Game Hub lists it as Drive Hub · Ferry.
