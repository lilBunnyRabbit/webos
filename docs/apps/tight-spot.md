# Drive Hub

Id `tight-spot` · Source: `src/lib/apps/user/tight-spot/` (`core/` the shared driving code, `hub/` the yard, `games/{id}/` one folder per game) · [ADR-087](../adrs/087-driving-hub.md) and [its brainstorm](../brainstorm/2026-10-28_driving-hub.md), [ADR-086](../adrs/086-tight-spot.md) and [the parking brainstorm](../brainstorm/2026-10-28_tight-spot.md)

## What it does

A hub for top-down driving games on one driving core: vehicles that steer like real ones at low speed (they turn about the rear axle, the front swings wide, the turning circle comes from the wheelbase and the steering lock), polygon collision, a following camera, a steering wheel and pedals on touch, parking sensors. The app opens in a yard: each game is a building to drive into, a garage pad swaps the vehicle, and the yard is a playground where nothing is judged. The garage holds 41 vehicles: cars (hatchback, city car, SUV, limousine, van, pickup, camper van), service vehicles (taxi, police car, ambulance, fire engine, ladder truck, garbage truck, baggage train), buses (city bus, double-decker, coach, articulated bus), trucks (box truck, semi-trailer truck, truck and drawbar trailer, road train), towing rigs (box trailer, caravan, boat, horse box, tractor and farm trailer, tractor and two trailers, quad and trailer), machines (forklift, skid-steer loader, telehandler, wheel loader, articulated dump truck, zero-turn mower, tank) and fun ones (tuk-tuk, golf cart, ice resurfacer, shopping trolley, F1 car). They steer five ways: front, rear, all-wheel (with a crab mode on the telehandler), skid (pivots on the spot) and frame (bends in the middle). Two games so far. The **Warehouse** moves pallets with a rear-steering forklift (below). **Tight Spot** is parking: spots generated from a seed (parallel at a kerb, nose in, reverse in, angled, a garage up to the wall, bays between pillars, a narrow aisle, a tight garage door, a dead-end turnaround, the end bay of a full dead-end aisle, a bus stop, a bus depot, a driveway, a slipway, a camping pitch and a loading dock), each with the vehicles the planner proves it with, a random spot of a picked kind (or any) with a seeded daily, a 48-level ladder, and a 3 × 3 open lot where any free bay counts. The app was Tight Spot before the hub; its id stayed `tight-spot`, so achievements, the daily and links carried over.

Three layers, each replaceable on its own (the parking brainstorm's later steps):

| Layer | Files | Replaced by |
|-------|-------|-------------|
| Sim | `core/vehicle.ts` (model), `core/collision.ts`, `core/obstacles.ts`, `games/parking/game.ts` and `games/forklift/game.ts` (rounds, judging) | a dynamic tyre model behind the same `step()` |
| World | `core/scene.ts` (shapes), `games/parking/world.ts` (chunks, plans), `lots.ts` (layouts), `kinds.ts` (kinds) | streamed chunks for the infinite world |
| Drawing | `core/draw.ts` (palette, ground, markers), `core/draw-vehicles.ts` (bodies, liveries, trailers), `games/parking/draw.ts` (the frame), `core/draw-math.ts`, `core/camera.ts` | a tilted 3D renderer |

`games/parking/game.svelte` runs a round on the core components; `core/sensors.ts` and `core/sound.ts` are the parking sensors and the sounds; `core/precision.ts` is the precision score both games use; `core/planner.ts` is test-only for parking (with `games/parking/poses.ts`) and works out the forklift's par time in a worker.

How to add a game, what the core gives and every gotcha met so far: [Drive Hub games](drive-hub-games.md).

## Structure

```
tight-spot/
├── index.ts               define_app: name Drive Hub, props `game`, `daily` and `mode`, a daily per game, the games' achievements
├── tight-spot.app.svelte  the shell: the yard or the active game, the switcher, per-window shared state
├── core/                  the driving core every game builds on
├── hub/                   the yard: layout (yard.ts), drawing (draw.ts, icons.ts), the component (yard.svelte)
└── games/
    ├── define.ts          define_game and GameProps
    ├── index.ts           the registry (GAMES) and the `game` prop's values
    ├── parking/           Tight Spot: index.ts (define_game), game.svelte (lazy), its model and tests
    └── forklift/          Warehouse: index.ts, game.svelte (lazy), warehouse.ts, game.ts, plan.ts, par.worker.ts, draw.ts, proofs
```

**The game contract.** `games/{id}/index.ts` default-exports `define_game({ id, name, icon, building, achievements, load })`: `icon` a Phosphor icon (the switcher and the doorway card show it), `building` how its building looks in the yard (`Building.CAR_PARK`, `Building.WAREHOUSE`), `achievements` merged into the manifest's list (ids stay unique across games; parking kept its old ids), `load` the lazy `game.svelte`. `games/index.ts` lists the games in yard order, and `UPCOMING` the names shut buildings after them carry (empty now both plots have a game). A game component takes `GameProps`: `settings` (the shared `DriveSettings`), `daily` (the day of the daily the app was opened with, given only to the game it names), `menu` (the switcher snippet, first in its toolbar), `play` (the window's Game Hub tracker; a game reports its daily results through `play.daily`) and `onexit` (back to the yard). It builds from the core components and keeps its own state; nothing is shared through a context. Each game stores under `app.tight-spot.{game}.*`.

**Core components.** `drive-frame.svelte` is a game's whole frame: toolbar, status bar and board (`toolbar`, `overlay` and `status` snippets), the canvas with its device-pixel resize observer, the theme palette, wheel and pinch zoom, the touch steering (wheel or buttons) and pedals, the keys from anywhere in the window, and the board focus. The game binds `size`, `palette`, `zoom` and the boxes its camera keeps clear of, and passes `engage` (a touch control was pressed; may start the round), `onpress` (the first pointer on a pedal), `onsteer`, `onkeydown` and `onrelease`. `drive-input.svelte.ts` (`DriveInput`) holds the keys, the held controls and the wheel and turns them into the model's `Input`; `loop.ts` (`frame_loop`) splits each animation frame into physics steps; `keys.ts` has the key maps; `gear-strip.svelte`, `speed-chip.svelte`, `round-card.svelte`, `key-hint.svelte` and `settings-panel.svelte` are the HUD and card pieces; `settings.svelte.ts` (`DriveSettings`) the settings every game shares; `obstacles.ts` the bounding-box broad phase over a scene's solids (`collide`); `scene.ts` the shapes (solids, bays, marks, surfaces).


## The hub

### Shell

`tight-spot.app.svelte` holds which view is up (`yard` or a game id), loads a game's component on first use (kept for the window), and owns the per-window state the views share: the `DriveSettings`, the yard's vehicle (a bus picked in the garage is still there after a game), and the game just left. Opening the app shows the yard with the car at the gate. The `game` prop (`yard`, `parking`, `forklift`) opens a game straight away (a value with no registered game opens the yard); `daily` opens that day's daily of the game `mode` names (`parking` or `forklift`, what Game Hub passes), else of a daily game in `game`, else parking's. `#/app/tight-spot?game=parking` and `app launch tight-spot game=parking` pass the same prop. Entering a game stores its id in `app.tight-spot.last`. While a game's chunk loads the shell shows its own toolbar (the switcher and the game's name) over "Opening …"; a chunk that fails to load (an update pruned the old assets) reports an error toast and goes back to the yard. Play time is counted by the shell (`track_play`, one Game Hub session per window), so time in the yard and in every game counts, and switching games doesn't start a new session; parking reports its daily through the `play` prop.

The switcher is the **Games** button first in every toolbar (a signpost and the word Games; below the `@md` container width only the signpost, named Games for screen readers): a system menu (`dialogs.menu`, a bottom sheet on phones) with the Yard and each game, the current one disabled and the last one played labelled "· last". Back (`window.on_back`) in a game closes its settings, then pauses a running round, then returns to the yard, where the vehicle comes out of that game's doorway facing away. Back in the yard closes the settings, then leaves the app.

### The yard

`hub/yard.ts`, pure and fixed: a 56 × 44 m walled yard (`build_yard(games)`). Two buildings on the north wall, 18 × 11.6 m, each with a 6 m wide, 5 m deep doorway cut into its front: the car park (Tight Spot) and the Warehouse. A building with a registered game is open; one past the last game would have its shutter down (a solid across the doorway, drawn as slats, labelled with the coming game's place from `UPCOMING`, and Soon), which no plot uses now. The gate is a gap in the south wall closed by a bar between two posts. A row of five bays runs along the west wall with a car and a van in two of them, four cones make a slalom across the middle (at y 22.5–23.5, two either side of the line in from the gate, so a 24 m road train backed up to the gate clears them), three bollards stand in the south-east (`bollards`, east of the line a road train takes out of the east doorway), and the garage pad (7.5 × 17 m, floor surface) is on the east side. Everything that stops the vehicle is in `solids`; the core draws `props` (walls, gate posts, parked cars), the yard draws the buildings (`structures`, a roof deck with painted bays for a car park, a ribbed roof with two skylights for a warehouse), the cones and bollards and the signs. A cone is a square base in translucent warning paint, a round body with an ink rim, a light band and a light tip; a bollard a round post with a warning band. Both are drawn bigger than they are when the view is far out (at least about 6 px across the body), capped so neighbours never merge. The signs are each building's name on its roof in the UI font, about 2.2 m tall (13 to 44 px, shrunk to fit the roof), with the game's Phosphor icon in the primary colour (`hub/icons.ts` mounts the icon into a detached node and turns its SVG into an image the canvas draws; a redraw follows its decode); a shut building's name is faint with Soon under it. An open doorway is lit: a primary-colour gradient from the back of the recess (`recess`) out over the apron, and a lamp strip along its back wall.

- **Doorway.** `door_at` is the open building whose doorway plus a 4 m apron in front holds the vehicle's nose (the middle of its front bumper). Doorways are outlined; the last game's and the one the vehicle is in glow in the primary colour. In one, a card offers the game (icon, name, Enter); Enter or the button enters it.
- **Welcome.** The first visit shows a card explaining the yard until the first input, which stores `app.tight-spot.yard_intro`; the guide (?) holds the same help after that.
- **Garage.** `on_pad` is the vehicle's body centre inside the pad; only a rig longer than the pad (17 m, a road train) also counts with its middle on it, since its vehicle sits past the pad when the rig is centred there. A caravan on the pad with its car off it doesn't count. On it a card shows the vehicles shelf by shelf (`GARAGE`: Cars, Service, Buses, Trucks, Towing, Machines, Fun, a Segmented laid out four to a row over the vehicles' buttons; it opens on the current vehicle's shelf), so a phone's card holds a handful at a time; picking one puts it on the pad's centre facing north (`pad_car`). Every vehicle is on exactly one shelf.
- **Placement.** `place(centre, spec)` puts a rig by its middle, front bumper to the trailer's tail. `gate_car` backs the rig up to 1.6 m inside the gate facing in; `door_car` puts it 0.5 m past a doorway's apron facing out.
- **Bump.** `drive` runs the model's `step`; a step that would touch a solid, or fold a trailer past the jackknife angle, doesn't happen: the vehicle keeps its pose at speed 0 (the wheel and the pedals still move; a frame steer's bend is its shape, so it keeps that too), and the touch point comes back. The yard plays one thunk when a touch starts and draws the red rings fading over 0.8 s. Backing away works on the next step.
- **Loop.** The yard has nothing to pause, so its frame loop runs only while something moves (speed, a held key or control, the front wheels still turning toward where the wheel points, a fading ring, Creep in gear) and sleeps until the next input; a resize, the theme or a gear change redraws once. The steering check matters standing still: a flicked touch wheel or C would otherwise leave the wheels, guide lines and Wheel° short of the wheel until Gas.
- **Camera.** Until the first input it frames the whole yard, with a 1 m margin, in the part the HUD, the card and the touch controls leave clear (`fit_span`), so every building and the car show on landing. From the first input it eases to following the vehicle with the round's look-ahead, kept inside the yard, at 34 m across the board's shorter side, wider than a round's 26 m, zoomed by vehicle length both ways like a round (`view_stretch`): more for a long rig, less for a short one (down to 0.6 × 34 m for the trolley). Back from a game it follows from the start. A card sits at the bottom, or on the left of a wide board; on a phone held sideways it stays at the bottom between the wheel and the pedals.

`hub/icons.browser.test.ts`: an icon becomes a drawable image in the given colour. `core/camera.test.ts` covers `fit_span` on a tall board with a card and between side controls. `hub/yard.test.ts`: every vehicle fits at the gate, out of each doorway (past its apron) and on the pad; the pad touches no solid; the garage shelves hold every vehicle once; a shut building has a solid in its doorway and offers nothing; the planner drives the hatchback from the gate into each doorway and onto the pad; a car driven into a cone stops clear of it with the touch point, and backs away again.

## Core

### The car

`vehicle.ts`. SI units, metres and seconds. `VehicleSpec.drive` says how a vehicle steers ([ADR-088](../adrs/088-vehicle-models.md)), a tagged union: `ackermann` with `steer_axle` `front` (every car, bus and rig), `rear` (the forklift), `all` (the ladder truck) or `crab` (the telehandler); `skid` with the track the turn feels; `frame` with the joint's place. `step()` stays the one entry and dispatches on it; `travel(placement, steer, distance, spec, crab)` is the same motion without the pedals, for the planner and the guide lines. `heading` 0 is east and grows clockwise on screen (y points down), so a positive steer turns right on every drive: steered at the rear, the rear wheels point left and the tail swings out. `car.steer` is the wheel angle on Ackermann, the wheel's share (−1 to 1) on a skid steer, the joint's bend on a frame steer; `max_steer` is its limit, and the touch wheel and Wheel° read it as a share of that.

- **Front and rear steer.** The pose is the centre of the axle that doesn't steer: the rear axle's for front steer, the front axle's for rear steer. Without slip that point only moves along the heading and the vehicle turns about a point on that axle's line, so the arc and the curvature `tan(δ) / L` are the same for both; only where the body, the wheels and the bumpers sit relative to the pose changes (`axles()`, `body_ends()`).
- **All-wheel steer** (`ALL_STEER`). The rear wheels counter-steer as far as the front ones turn, so the turn centre sits level with the middle of the wheelbase: the pose is there, and the curvature is `tan(δ) / (L/2)` (`steer_base()`), half the circle of the same truck steered only in front. `max_steer` derives δ the same way with `L/2`. Both pairs are drawn at their own angles (`atan2(x·κ, 1 − y·κ)`, negative `x` behind).
- **Crab steer** (`CRAB_STEER`). An all-wheel steer with a crab mode: `Car.crab` (on only for such a vehicle, set by `set_crab`, which refuses above `park_speed` like P) turns both axles the same way, so the body slides along the wheels at `heading + steer` without turning (`crab_move`); every wheel is drawn at `steer`, and `turn_centre` is null. Off, it is an all-wheel steer. The pose is the middle in both modes, so switching doesn't move the body.
- **Skid steer** (`{ kind: "skid", track }`). Two sides driven apart: `speed` is what the outer side covers, and the wheel's share `s` slows the inner one, down to running it backwards at full lock. The body moves `distance · (1 − |s|)` along the heading and turns `2 · distance · s / track` (`skid_move`, exact for the step), so half lock turns about the inner side, which stands still, and full lock pivots on the spot as soon as there is gas, the two sides rolling opposite ways. `track` is wider than the wheels' or the tracks' (1.6 m on the bobcat's 1.32 m), the way they scrub. The pose is the middle of the contact; the mower's contact is its drive axle (`wheelbase` 0), so it pivots about the rear wheels and its deck swings round. `max_steer` is 1.
- **Frame steer** (`{ kind: "frame", pivot }`). Two frames on a joint `pivot` behind the front axle; the pose is the rear axle and `car.steer` the bend γ. Neither axle slides, so the rear frame turns at `dθ = (v·sin γ − a·dγ) / (b·cos γ + a)` (`a` the front axle to the joint, `b` the joint to the rear axle) and the front axle covers `v·cos γ + b·dθ·sin γ`: bending at a standstill swings both frames about their axles, which stay put. `frame_move` integrates that with RK4 in pieces of at most 5 cm or 1.1°, with the bend and the speed changing linearly over the step. Held bent the rear axle circles at `sin γ / (b·cos γ + a)`. `max_steer` solves for the bend at which the outer wheel of the axle that swings widest draws `turning_radius` (40° on the loader, 47° on the dump truck). `front_frame()` is the front axle as a pose, `frame_ends()` each frame's extent along its own axis (a 0.12 m gap either side of the joint), `bodies()` the two frames, `headings()` both frames' headings, `nose()` the front bumper's middle.

The hatchback (`HATCHBACK`): 4.3 m long, 1.8 m wide, 2.6 m wheelbase, 0.8 m rear overhang (0.9 m front), 1.55 m track. Its `turning_radius` is 5.3 m kerb to kerb, the circle the outer front wheel draws at full lock. `max_steer` derives the bicycle-model lock from it: the outer front wheel sits at `sqrt(R² − L²)` from the turn centre sideways, half a track out from the rear axle's centre, so `δ = atan(L / (sqrt(R² − L²) − track/2))` = 34.1°, and the rear axle's centre turns on a 3.84 m circle.

`step(car, input, dt)` is the kinematic (no-slip) bicycle model, stepped at 120 Hz or finer: each animation frame is split into equal steps of at most 1/120 s covering exactly its own time (capped at 100 ms; a frame stamped before the loop started, or a zero step, moves nothing, since `Infinity · 0` in P would make the speed NaN), since fixed steps against a 120 Hz screen land 0, 1 or 2 to a frame and the car visibly shakes:

1. The wheel moves toward the wanted angle at up to `STEER_BOOST` (1.6) × `max_steer / steer_time`, at a standstill too: `steer_time` (1.1 s for the hatchback) is a steady hold's centre to lock, and the wheel itself can go about 0.7 s, so a held key that speeds up (Controls) isn't clipped and a touch wheel drag follows with less lag. With `steer: null` (hands off) the target is straight ahead and the rate is the steady one scaled with speed up to 2 m/s, so the wheel self-centres only while rolling, like caster.
2. Pedals ramp (3/s down, 8/s up) so a key press isn't an on/off jump.
3. In D or R the car creeps: acceleration `creep_accel · (1 − v/creep_speed)`, or the throttle's `accel · throttle · (1 − v/top)`, whichever is larger. The creep is off by default: the app passes `NO_CREEP` (`creep_accel` 0) to `advance` unless the Creep setting (`app.tight-spot.creep`) is on, so the car moves only on the gas; rolling resistance and the brake take speed off without crossing zero. P holds the car still; N coasts. `shift` refuses P above 0.3 m/s.
4. The pose moves along the exact arc for the step's distance (`arc()`), curvature `tan(δ) / L`, so the rear axle never slides sideways and a full-lock circle closes on itself.

The steered wheels are drawn at their Ackermann angles (`wheels()`): a wheel `x` along from the pose and `y` across points at `atan2(x·κ, 1 − y·κ)`, which for front steer is the familiar `atan(L / (1/κ ∓ track/2))`. `atan2` matters for rear steer: a forklift's turn centre at full lock lies inside its track, so its inner rear wheel turns past 90°. The dashed guide lines are `sweep()`: the two leading corners' paths for 5 m at the current wheel angle.

Each wheel's rolled distance is in `car.roll` (metres, signed, in `wheels()` order), for the tread drawing. `roll()` adds the step's distance times the wheel's own radius over the rear axle centre's: a wheel at `(x, y)` from the rear axle's centre turns on `|κ| · hypot(x, 1/κ − y)` of it, which is `hypot(x·κ, 1 − y·κ)` and 1 driving straight. On lock the outer front wheel rolls the most and the inner rear the least; standing still nothing rolls, whatever the wheel does.

The **articulated bus** is the one-trailer model: the turntable is the ball, the bellows the drawbar (0.65 m), the rear section the trailer (axle 6.0 m behind the turntable), so it folds like a trailer and jackknifes at the same 70°. The trailers: horse box (ball 1.1 m behind the axle, 3.3 m to the trailer's axle), farm trailer (1.0, 3.6), small trailer (0.45, 1.5), boat (1.0, 4.0; the boat runs 2.6 m past the axle), caravan (1.0, 4.8, wider than the car that tows it).

**The chains** (wave 2). The **semi-trailer**'s kingpin sits 0.35 m ahead of the tractor's drive axle (`hitch: -0.35`), its box reaches 1.6 m past the kingpin over the fifth wheel (`drawbar: -1.6`), and its tri-axle group turns about its middle, 7.7 m behind the kingpin; it folds 80°. The **drawbar trailer** is two hinges: the drawbar pivots on the truck's coupling 2.75 m behind its rear axle and steers a dolly 2.7 m further back (60°), and the body turns on a turntable over the dolly's axle (`hitch: 0`, 45°), its tandem 5 m behind. The **road train** is an A-double: a 9.5 m semi on the tractor (80°), a converter dolly hooked 2.3 m behind that semi's axle (60°), and a second 9.5 m semi on the dolly's fifth wheel (80°). The **baggage train**'s carts each hook 0.3 m behind the one ahead (75°), and the **tractor with two trailers** hooks the second farm trailer 0.4 m behind the first's box (70°). Every one is in the garage; the box truck, the semi, the drawbar truck and the road train park at the loading dock (the box truck also at the bus stop and the depot); the baggage train and the tractor with two trailers are garage-only until their games come.

**The fun ones** (wave 4) are plain front-steer specs, no model change. The **tuk-tuk** has one front wheel on the centre line: the model's front pair stands in for it (the bicycle model steers the same), so `turning_radius` is still measured at the outer front corner of the track and only the drawing shows one wheel (its roll is the pair's mean, its angle the bicycle angle `steer`). The **golf cart**, the **trolley** and the **F1 car** have no creep (`creep_accel: 0`; `creep_speed` stays 1, since 0 divides 0 by 0 in `acceleration()`). The **ice resurfacer** is slow and heavy: 3.5 m/s, 1.5 back. The **shopping trolley** is a joke: every caster of a real one swivels, so it is front steer on a huge lock that pivots it almost on the spot, at walking pace (1.6 m/s, 1.2 back). The **F1 car** has a tiny lock, so its 9.5 m turning circle is a lorry's, with 6 m/s² of pull, 12 m/s² of brake and a 14 m/s cap (what the no-slip model still drives believably). `VehicleSpec.unlit` (the trolley and the F1 car) skips the headlight beams.

**The forklift.** Steered at the rear (`REAR_STEER`), so its pose is the front axle's centre; at full lock the turn centre lies just inside the inner front wheel, the forks barely move and the counterweight swings wide. `VehicleSpec.forks` (`{ length, width }`, 1.2 × 0.86 m) puts the tines ahead of the body: `fork_corners()` is their box, `bodies()` returns the body, then the forks, then a trailer, so collision, sensors (the front one reads from the fork tips, `rig_ends()`) and the planner (`inflate` grows the forks' width) all see them. **The machines** (wave 3) are the vehicles that drive differently. The **skid-steer loader** (bobcat), **zero-turn mower** and **tank** spin on the spot; the bobcat and the tank pivot about their middle, the mower about its drive wheels. The site machines beep reversing; the mower has no creep, like the golf cart. ### Trailers and hitch chains

`VehicleSpec.hitches` is the hitch chain ([ADR-088](../adrs/088-vehicle-models.md)): empty for a solo vehicle, one `TrailerSpec` for a car and trailer, several for a train. Each link pivots on the body ahead of it: `hitch` is from that body's axle (the vehicle's pose for the first link, the link before's axle after that) back to the hinge, negative when the hinge sits ahead of the axle (a semi's kingpin); `axle` the hinge to the link's own axle; `drawbar` the hinge to the front of its box, negative when the box reaches past the hinge; `max_angle` how far that hinge may fold. `BOX_TRAILER`: the ball 1.0 m behind the rear axle (h), the trailer's axle 2.45 m behind the ball (L), a 1.1 m drawbar to a 2.5 × 1.6 m box, a 1.45 m track. `Car.trailers` holds one `{ heading, roll }` per link (φ from its axle toward its hinge, and its two wheels' rolled distance), absent on a solo vehicle; `create_car` puts every link straight behind.

`step()` stays the one entry: after the vehicle's arc it calls `tow()`, which integrates the chain link by link with the standard one-trailer kinematics. With ψ = φₚ − φ the link's hinge angle against the body ahead (θ, the vehicle's heading, for the first), and v and ω that body's axle speed and turn per metre s the vehicle covers (1 and κ = tan δ / L for the vehicle itself):

```
dφ/ds = (v·sin ψ − h·ω·cos ψ) / L            (for the first link: (sin ψ − h·κ·cos ψ) / L)
dA/ds = v·cos ψ + h·ω·sin ψ                    (the distance the link's axle covers: its v for the next link)
```

θ along the arc is exact (θ₀ + κs), so RK4 over the whole chain in pieces of at most 2.5 cm stays accurate even reversing, which amplifies every error. The first link's arithmetic is the one the single trailer always had, so one-trailer rigs move bit for bit as before. Forward, every ψ decays over about its L of travel; reversing, each grows at the same rate and feeds the next, so a chain diverges faster than one trailer and a long one can't be backed at all. The wheels roll the axle's distance plus or minus half a track times the turn. A step of no time changes nothing, trailers included. `trailer_poses()` gives every link's axle as a pose, `trailer_pose`, `trailer_corners`, `trailer_wheels` and `hitch_point` take the link's index, and `hitch_angles()` every hinge's fold.

A hinge folded past its link's `max_angle` (`jackknife()` gives the first such hinge, `jackknifed()` whether any) ends the round like a contact: `hit.kind = "jackknife"`, the ring at that hinge, and the card says "Jackknifed the trailer", or on a chain "Jackknifed at the second hinge" (`hit.hinge`). Every trailer so far folds 70° at the ball, the articulated bus's turntable included.

Tests (`vehicle.test.ts`) pin the steering: lock in `steer_time / STEER_BOOST`, a target moved at the steady rate followed without lag, `steer_boost` flat for a short hold then rising monotonically to the cap, and the caster centring at the steady rate. And the geometry: the outer front wheel's circle is 5.3 m, the rear axle stays on its 3.84 m circle for 2000 steps, it only ever moves along the heading, the outer front corner swings outside the rear wheels' path, reversing on lock retraces the same circle, plus the steering rate, creep, brake hold, P refusal and determinism; every vehicle's lock gives its own turning radius; towing forward straightens the trailer, reversing grows the hitch angle until it jackknifes, the trailer's axle never slides sideways, its wheels roll the right distances, and towing is deterministic. A three-link chain starts straight with every link a body and the tail at `rig_ends`, its first link moves exactly as a lone trailer, forward straightens every link, reversing makes the hinges diverge until one folds past its limit, no link's axle slides sideways, each hinge jackknifes at its own limit, and it is deterministic with a step of no time (or less) a no-op. Rear steer: the pose is the front axle and the rear wheels point against the turn, full lock gives the turning radius at the outer rear wheel while the front axle stays on its circle, steering right turns right with the front axle only moving along the heading, the tail swings wider than anything at the front, each wheel rolls its own arc (the outer rear the most), and it is deterministic with a step of no time a no-op.

### Drawing

`draw.ts` draws one frame onto the canvas in world metres; `read_palette` resolves the theme once per theme change.

- **Contrast on any body.** The default theme's primary (the player's paint) is as light as its foreground, so nothing mixed from the foreground shows on it. Detail on a car takes the theme's two extremes instead: `ink`, the darker of foreground and background-darkest, and `glow`, the lightest of foreground, background-lightest and cottontail (lightness read with `oklch(from … l 0 0)`). A lamp is a `glow` (or `error`) lens in an `ink` housing, so one of the two stands off any body. Glass and roof take whichever of `ink` and the plain light tone (no cottontail) is further in lightness from that paint (`against()`), mixed into it. Parked cars' paints are mixed with the background, not with transparent, so lines don't show through them.
- **Lights.** Headlights, tail lights (brighter with a red halo while braking), reverse lamps in R. In D or R the lamps throw a soft cone on the ground (a linear gradient from `glow` to clear, shorter behind), only when the ground is much darker than `glow`; on light themes it read as a grey smear.
- **Tyres.** Drawn over the body, slightly wider than real and nudged out, so the front pair's Ackermann angles read. Tread stripes every 11 cm come from `tread(roll, pitch, length)`: seen from above it's the top of the tyre, which moves the way the car does, so stripes run forward in D and back in R. From 1.2 m/s they fade, gone by 3 m/s, the way a spinning wheel blurs; left on, they strobe at 60 Hz and read as the car shaking.
- **Shadows.** Every car casts its body shape offset by a fixed world vector (the sun doesn't turn with the car), in `ink` at 30 %, longer the taller the style (`HEIGHT`: a quad 0.6, a hatchback 1, a coach 1.75, a double-decker 2.6).
- **Clean lines.** Pieces of one kind (each ground surface, kerbs, walls, shelves, pillars) go in one path, every piece wound the same way (`wound()`), so abutting pieces fill without anti-aliasing seams and a translucent stroke never doubles where two meet. Outlines are stroked before the fill (the fill hides the inner half and every shared edge) with round joins, so corners don't spike. Bay lines and stop lines are one stroke per kind; road centre dashes are phased by where each segment starts along its line (`dash_phase()`), so the pattern runs on across chunks. Mark widths have a floor of 1.25 CSS px when zoomed out. The world transform is not snapped to whole device pixels: snapping made the moving car jitter a pixel against the camera.

### Collision

`collision.ts`, hand-written. Every body is tested: the vehicle and, towing, each trailer's box down the chain (`bodies()`); a drawbar isn't a body. `overlaps` is the separating-axis test for two convex polygons: they're apart if some edge normal of either one splits their projections. Touching counts as apart, so a car whose side lies on a wall's face isn't a crash. Every solid is convex (a rectangle or a rotated rectangle), so nothing is decomposed. `core/obstacles.ts` keeps each scene's solids with their bounding boxes (`obstacles`, cached per world) and runs SAT only on boxes that meet the car's (`collide`).

`contact_point` is where the red ring goes: the average of the corners of each shape inside the other and the edge crossings.

### Sensors

`sensors.ts`: for the bumper the gear drives toward, each solid's edges are taken into the car's frame, clipped to the strip the car would sweep driving straight (half a width plus 5 cm each side) and to 1.6 m ahead; the nearest point is the reading. Towing, the rear sensor is the last trailer's (`sensor_frame`): its frame, its width and its tail (1.15 m behind its axle on the box trailer), and the arcs are drawn there. `sound.ts` beeps a 1750 Hz tone, quicker the closer (`beep_period`), steady under 30 cm, from an `AudioContext` made on first use. The readout in the corner and the arcs at the bumper go from warning to error under 50 cm. The same `Sounds` plays the other cues, all behind the Sound setting: a quiet tick on a gear change, a falling thunk on a touch, two rising notes when parked and a third for a new best.

### Controls

Keyboard: arrows or WASD turn the wheel and work the pedals by direction. The wheel turns at the spec's steady rate (`steer_time`). Only winding it back from the other side speeds up: held while the wheel still points the opposite way, it eases up to `STEER_BOOST` times that (`steer_boost(held, against)`: 1 until 0.25 s, then a smoothstep to 1.6 over 0.3 s), and drops back to the steady rate once the wheel passes centre, so turning toward a side is always the fine rate. Letting go or flipping the direction starts over. The touch ◀ ▶ buttons run through the same `step_wheel`, so they ramp the same way. Pedals: ↑/W is gas in D (and P or N) and the brake in R, ↓/S the other way round; a fresh press (not a key repeat) while the car stands below 0.05 m/s shifts to that direction (`choose_direction`), so braking to a stop and pressing again backs up; E, R or Q, N, P or Space pick gears; C centres the wheel; X switches crab mode on a vehicle that has one (`CRAB_KEY`); + and − zoom. Escape pauses and resumes; Enter starts, resumes, and after a round runs the card's main button (`primary_action`); R tries again after a round. Closing the settings hands the focus back to the board, and a key that lands on the page body (after the guide closes) is routed to the board while the window is in front. Brake from P at the start reverses. The one gear control is the P R N D strip on the board's top edge, 44 px targets on touch; the speed then stacks over the sensor readout at the top right so the strip has room. Pinch to zoom. Each control takes its own pointer, so steering and the pedals work at once.

A phone held sideways (`pointer: coarse` and the app's own `ts-app` size container wider than 1.25 × its height and under 30rem tall) folds the status bar away and tightens the toolbar to 4 px of padding, so the board gets about 30 px more of its ~300; the time and moves show as a chip next to the gear strip instead. ### World: chunks and plans

### Every spot can be reached

`planner.ts` is a hybrid A*: nodes keep exact poses, the closed set is a grid of 0.25 m, 5°, each hinge's angle in 11° steps (none without a trailer; a chain's key is a string, since its further hinges would overflow a safe integer) and direction. A move (`Move`: `steer`, the car's own steer value, `distance` and an optional `crab`) holds the wheel for 0.6 m, forwards and backwards, at five steering shares up to full lock; a move that runs into something, or jackknifes, stops short at the last clear 0.15 m, the way a driver creeps up to a bumper. Each 0.15 m piece runs through `drive()`, which is `travel()`: the same `arc()` and `tow()` as `step()`, so every link's angle is integrated along every path, reversing included. Per drive: a skid steer has nine shares (±1, ±0.5, ±0.25, ±0.1, 0), so full lock is a pivot on the spot (0.6 m of track is 49° for the bobcat) and the small ones are gentle curves; a crab-capable vehicle adds four crab moves (±0.5 and ±1 of lock); a frame steer's move is a bend toward the share at `FRAME_RAMP` (0.5 rad per metre rolled, about 3 m lock to lock), so the bend is continuous along a path and part of the node, and the closed set bins it in place of a hitch, in 22.5° steps (11° split the set so finely that a dump truck took 20–30 s a seed). Front, rear and all-wheel steer keep their five arcs. Cost is distance (reverse × 1.3) plus 2.5 per change of direction, heuristic the straight distance plus 3 m per radian of heading error (the vehicle's, plus each trailer's), weighted 4. `inflate` grows every link's box. The goal is within `reach` and `heading_tolerance` of `to`, or any test the caller passes as `arrive`.

`proof.ts` (test-only, like `poses.ts`) proves a spot two ways, both with the bodies inflated by 5 cm:

- **From the start.** The search starts from the car exactly as `create_game` places it, a trailer straight behind, aims at a parked pose (`parked_poses`: offsets inside the bay that count as parked and keep 5 cm clear) and stops at the first placement `parked_in` accepts. The test then drives the moves again from that start in the same 15 cm pieces with the real bodies and asserts no contact, no jackknife and `verdict()` "inside". That's what a player can do, the unstable trailer reverse included. Trailer kinds and all 48 levels go this way.
- **Out of the bay.** From a parked pose to within 1.5 m and 30° of the start; the model has no slip, so the same path backwards gets in from there. It's cheaper, and only sound without a trailer (reversing one from a hitch angle a hair off the path's folds away), so it's used for the seed sweeps of car and bus kinds.

A towed rig at the bus stop goes in forwards and pulls up straight, so its proof searches forward only and arrives once the front section is within 3° of the bay and driving straight ahead from there (up to 20 m, in 15 cm pieces, clear of everything) ends with the game calling it parked (`pull_up`); the straight run is added to the moves before they're driven again from the start. A forward-only search alone can't line both sections up (its 11° hitch bins merge the states that would), and with reversing allowed the 18 m bus took 10–30 s a seed from the road and failed some. At the depot the articulated bus gets the plain search from the start, 1–10 s a seed.

`planner.test.ts` sweeps every kind with each vehicle its levels pick from (`ladder`, or all of them for the camping pitch) at difficulty 0, 0.5 and 1 over 8 seeds, and every level. The vehicles a kind gained since (`added()`: a look on a spec already proven there is skipped) are swept beside it in two files so they run in parallel: `planner-vehicles.test.ts` the solo ones over 8 seeds, `planner-rigs.test.ts` the towing rigs over 2, each seed a search from the start with the trailer. Per seed on the owner's machine (2 seeds a test, the worst difficulty): the slipway boat about 5 s (10.3 s at difficulty 0), the driveway horse box about 4 s (7.3 s at 0; 15.9 s once under load from another run), the quad's twitchy little trailer about 4 s (8.3 s at 1), the articulated bus at the depot about 7 s (13.8 s at 0.5) and at the bus stop under 1 s (1.5 s at 0). The loading dock's levels and sweep use the semi, proven from its set-up start by a search in reverse only (one reverse in, 0.1–3.6 s a seed); its drawbar truck and road train are in `planner-rigs.test.ts`, forward only with the pull-up (the drawbar truck 1–4 s a seed, the road train 1–7 s), and the box truck in `planner-vehicles.test.ts`. The machines are in `planner-vehicles.test.ts` over 8 seeds, out of the bay like the cars (a skid, crab or frame path is as reversible as an arc): the dump truck is the slowest, 11–20 s a test at the depot and the loading dock while the other files run (about 1–3 s a seed alone), the bobcat and the mower in the angled bays 4–6 s, the rest under 5 s. The camping pitch is in `planner.test.ts` over 8 seeds, about 2.4 s a seed at difficulty 0 (18.8 s), the slowest test, against 120 s timeouts like every sweep; the app's Node tests take about 105 s in all.

The free bays of two open lots are covered through their lot's gate, in each of the open lot's vehicles (`planner-open-lot.test.ts`, its own file so it runs beside the spot sweeps). ### Judging

`game.ts`. Any contact ends the round (`advance` returns `crashed` with the contact point and what was hit). Towing, a hinge folded past its limit ends it too. Shifting into P checks `bay_for`: parked when every corner of every body (the car, and the trailer's box) is inside the bay polygon and the car's heading, the trailer's and a frame steer's front frame's (`headings()`), are within 5° of the bay's (or of its reverse when `either`). P anywhere else just stops the car, and a line at the bottom of the board says "Not inside a bay", "Not straight in the bay" or "Facing the wrong way" (within 5° of the reverse of a bay that isn't `either`) (`verdict`), but only once the car has been in gear, since a round starts in P.

Moves are changes between D and R, the first gear and N or P not counted. Stars: at or under the kind's par is three, two more is two, else one (pars: parallel 2, nose in 0, reverse 1, angled 0, garage 0, pillars 1, narrow 2, door 2, dead end 2, end bay 2, bus stop 1, depot 1, driveway 2, slipway 2, camping pitch 2, loading dock 2; open lot 1). Time is the sim's own clock (`ms`), so pauses and a hidden window don't count.

#### Precision

`precision(car, bay, spec)`, a score apart from the stars, which stay moves only. From every corner of every body (the car and, towing, the trailer's box) a ray runs to the bay's outline along the bay's axis, back, and across both ways; the shortest of each is that side's gap, so a crooked car or a trailer folded to one side shows in the gap it closes. The axis is the bay's heading, or its reverse when an `either` bay is entered the other way round.

- side offset `s = (right − left) / 2`, out of `(right + left) / 2`, the room either side when centred;
- depth offset `d = (front − rear) / 2`, out of `(front + rear) / 2`;
- angle `a`, the worse of the car's, the trailer's and a frame steer's front frame's heading against the axis, out of the 5° that still counts as parked.

Each error is its share `e` of that room, clamped to 0–1, and the score is `100 × (1 − e_s²)(1 − e_d²)(1 − e_a²)`: 100 dead centre and square, 0 against a line or 5° off, and falling as any one grows. A trailer rig is judged as one footprint, not by whichever body seems to matter: in a driveway, on a slipway or on a camping pitch both are in the bay and both have to be placed. Squaring the shares keeps small slips cheap (6 cm off in a bay with 45 cm either side is 98 %) and the edges expensive. The parked card shows "Precision N %" with ↔ the side offset, ↕ the depth offset (cm) and ∠ the angle; 95 % or more (rounded) adds a Perfect badge next to the stars and unlocks the Perfect park achievement. The bays are sized differently, so the same centimetres score differently by kind: a parallel gap or a bus stop box has metres of depth to spare, a garage with the shelf very little.

### The floor

`build_warehouse(seed, difficulty)`, pure. A walled floor; along the north wall a single rack row opening south (face A); one or two back-to-back rows below it (faces B and C, D and E), each face a row of 6–8 bays between 0.1 m uprights with a back rail; aisles between the rows; cross aisles at both ends with a 0.5 m pillar at each double row's end; a main aisle; three to six dock spots along the south wall, each in front of a dock door (Dock A…); the charging bay in the south-east corner, where the forklift starts facing north. `layout()` sets the sizes from the difficulty (all `lerp`s) and the seed:

| | Roomy (0) | Tight (1) |
|---|---|---|
| Aisle | 4.6 m (+0–0.25) | 4.0 m (+0–0.25) |
| Room either side of a pallet in a bay | 0.2 m | 0.1 m |
| Main aisle | 5.6 m (+0–0.3) | 4.8 m (+0–0.3) |
| Double rows | one (75 % of seeds below 0.3) | two |
| Stocked bays | 35 % | 70 % |

A pallet is 1.2 m along the forks by 1.0 m. A rack bay is 1.25 m deep to its back rail; its **slot** (the area a pallet counts as put in) runs from 0.2 m out in the aisle to 2 cm short of the rail, between the uprights, and a stored pallet sticks out 8 cm past the front line so the forklift's body stays clear of the uprights while its forks are in. A dock spot is the pallet with 0.3 m all round. `Slot.heading` is the way the forks point going in; `Slot.home` is where a stored pallet sits.

`build_floor(seed, difficulty, size)` adds the job: for each of `size` pallets a task (pick from a rack bay to a dock spot, put away from a dock spot to a bay, or move between faces), every slot used once, then stock in the other bays. Stock pallets are solids (`SolidKind.PALLET`); the job's pallets are not, since they move.

### Picking up and putting down

- **Forks in a pallet.** `fork_fit` measures the forklift against a pallet: the angle off its axis, how far its centre is off the forks' centre line, and the gap from the mast to its face. The forks are **in its pockets** only with both tines under it: within 5° (`JAM_ANGLE`, the lift's `MAX_ANGLE`) and with its centre at most `JAM_SIDE` = (pallet width − fork spread) / 2 = 7 cm off the forks' centre line; outside them, forks overlapping a pallet jam (contact, "Jammed the forks into a pallet"). At the full 5° a tine's tip can still reach a few centimetres past the pallet's side; a simple rule, not the exact footprint.
- **Lift** (`fork_action` → `lift`): standing (under 0.05 m/s), forks in the pockets (square within 5° by that rule) and fully in (mast within 15 cm of the face, `FULL_GAP`). The pallet then rides at the offset it was picked up with (`Load.carried`, the pallet's pose in the forklift's frame), and joins the bodies that collide.
- **Lower:** standing, the carried pallet's every corner inside the task's target slot and its heading within 5° of the slot's. It stays where it was put; the next task starts.
- **Contact** (`contact()`, the same function the planner uses with a margin): the body or the forks against walls, pillars, racks and stock (`collide_shape` over the cached obstacles), the body against any job pallet, the forks against one outside its pockets, then the carried pallet against everything; any of these ends the round (`by_load` says the load touched it: "The load touched a rack").
- `fork_hint` says why the Fork control is off once the forklift stands at the pallet: in the pockets "Drive the forks fully in"; short of it (tips not in yet, within 25° and 50 cm) and off the pockets "Line up: 6.5° off" or "Line up: pallet 9 cm to the left", before the tines go in and jam; or carries one within 2.2 m of the target ("Not straight…", "Not inside Dock C yet"); `fork_depth` is the HUD's *Forks N %*.

Tasks go in order; the HUD names the one in hand (`task_line`: "Pallet 2/3 · pick up at B4", then "Pallet 2/3 → Dock C"), the current pallet pulses, its number in a pill in the aisle at its open face (on the pallet when zoomed far out, hidden while the forks are in it), and the current task's target is outlined in the primary colour with a glow (pulsing while its pallet is on the forks) and a filled "→N" pill; later targets are dashed with an outlined "→N", done ones green; and the core's off-screen pointer leads to the pallet or, carrying, to the target.

### Scoring

- Stars (`stars`): one for the job done, one for moves (D↔R changes) at or under `par_moves` = 5 per pallet less 2 (in, out, in, out per pallet, the last needs no backing out, plus one spare each), one for the time at or under the par time.
- **Par time** (`par_of`): the planner drives the whole job from the real start (below); its path length at 1.4 m/s, plus 2.5 s per change of direction and 2 s per lift or lower, times 1.25 for slack, rounded up to 5 s. The planner's own changes of direction aren't the moves par: its fine steering shuffles more than a driver does. It runs in a worker per round (`work_out_par`), one to six seconds for four pallets, started once per round: the component keys it on `$derived(game.round)`, whose object only changes with a new round (Again reuses it, so the par is kept). A job is recorded the moment it's done, with the star for the job, the moves star and the time star when the par is already in; a job that ends first keeps waiting for its round's par (its worker isn't cancelled by Again, New or a level change) and is recorded again with the time star when it arrives and earns it. A planner that finds nothing (ruled out for every level and the sampled jobs) leaves the time star unearned.
- **Precision** per drop: `fit()` from `core/precision.ts` with the pallet's corners and heading against the target slot, as parking scores a car in a bay; the card shows the average (95 % or more is Perfect) and each drop.
- Best: more stars, then time (`record_best`).
- Levels (`level_round`): 16, four each of one to four pallets, difficulty 0 → 1, seeds `hash(0xf02c, index)`. Jobs (`job_round`): the picked size, difficulty 0.15–0.85 from the seed. Daily (`daily_round`): three pallets, difficulty 0.3–0.8, seed `daily_seed(day, "tight-spot/forklift")`.

### Every job can be done

`plan_job` chains hybrid A* legs from the forklift's start: to each pallet (forks fully and squarely in; the pick leg counts as lined up only with the tips still short of the pallet, 1° and 2 cm inside the pockets' limits, so the straight run in never leans on their edge), then with it to its target, every other pallet where the job has left it, the bodies grown by 5 cm through the same `contact()`. Each leg searches only until the forklift is lined up with the slot (within 5°, in the pockets or the target's side room, the slot ahead) and a straight run in reaches the goal; the run is appended (`straight_in`), so the search never has to hit a centimetre window. The planner gets the game's collision through `PlanOptions.blocked`, finer steering (nine shares of lock, `steering`) and a higher cost per change of direction (`switch_cost` 8), which halved its shuffles. `prove()` drives every leg again in 15 cm pieces with the real bodies and no margin and checks the lift at each pallet and the drop at each target. The proofs: all 16 levels (`planner-levels.test.ts`, `planner-levels-late.test.ts`, side by side, 0.7–10 s a level) and two random jobs of each size plus a fixed daily (`planner-jobs.test.ts`).

`warehouse.test.ts`: the same seed builds the same floor, difficulty tightens it, every slot's pallet fits it and touches nothing, the start is clear, a job uses distinct slots with free targets. `game.test.ts`: the fork rules, a carried pallet's offset, a whole one-pallet job through lift and lower with its precision, jams and contacts, moves, stars, bests, levels.

Played through in a browser from the arrow keys and F (the planner's level 1 turned into key presses by a closed-loop driver over `DriveInput` and `advance`, replayed under Playwright's fake clock): the pick, the carry and the drop, the Fork button lighting up, the done card and the achievement.

## Limits

- Kinematic only: no slip, so speed changes nothing about the path. Fine at parking speed; drift is the planned dynamic model.
- Playtested from Playwright on the owner's dev server, desktop and a 390 × 844 touch emulation; not on a real phone or with a real wheel feel review.
- The planner proves a path with 5 cm of clearance, not that a person finds it easy; difficulty ranges are tuned by eye.
- The open lot is fixed at 3 × 3 and only uses three kinds of lot.
- Precision isn't comparable across kinds: it's a share of each bay's room, and the rooms differ.
- The bus stop has no tapered lay-by: a 32 m lot has no room for one with cars at both ends.
- The yard is fixed with two building plots, both taken; a third game needs a bigger yard.
- The Warehouse's par time takes the planner one to six seconds in a worker; the card shows "…" meanwhile. A job finished first is recorded straight away and gets its time star when the par arrives; a second job finished before the first's par arrives replaces the wait, so the first never gets that star.
- Parking's dailies from before the per-game split stay under `tight-spot` in `plays.json`; Game Hub no longer shows them.
- The tractor, the tractor with two trailers and the baggage train have no spot yet. A chain doesn't collide with itself: a body folding into the one ahead shows overlapping until the hinge's limit ends the round. The coach only takes the depot (some bus-stop seeds failed).
- A contact in the yard stops the vehicle dead; nothing is pushed, cones included.
- A skid steer is kinematic like everything else: its sides scrub sideways only as far as the wider `track` stands in for, and it turns the same on any ground. The ladder truck is all-wheel steer, not a tiller truck with a second driver; the telehandler has no two-wheel mode. A skid steer, a frame steer or a vehicle crabbing tows nothing: `step` and `travel` move a chain only behind an Ackermann arc, and no such vehicle has one.
