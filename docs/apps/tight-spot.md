# Drive Hub

Id `tight-spot` · Source: `src/lib/apps/user/tight-spot/` (`core/` the shared driving code, `hub/` the yard, `games/{id}/` one folder per game) · [ADR-087](../adrs/087-driving-hub.md) and [its brainstorm](../brainstorm/2026-10-28_driving-hub.md), [ADR-086](../adrs/086-tight-spot.md) and [the parking brainstorm](../brainstorm/2026-10-28_tight-spot.md)

## What it does

A hub for top-down driving games on one driving core: vehicles that steer like real ones at low speed (they turn about the rear axle, the front swings wide, the turning circle comes from the wheelbase and the steering lock), polygon collision, a following camera, a steering wheel and pedals on touch, parking sensors. The app opens in a yard, a street with a building per game to drive into, a garage pad swaps the vehicle, and the yard is a playground where nothing is judged. The garage holds 42 vehicles: cars (hatchback, city car, SUV, GT car, limousine, van, pickup, camper van), service vehicles (taxi, police car, ambulance, fire engine, ladder truck, garbage truck, baggage train), buses (city bus, double-decker, coach, articulated bus), trucks (box truck, semi-trailer truck, truck and drawbar trailer, road train), towing rigs (box trailer, caravan, boat, horse box, tractor and farm trailer, tractor and two trailers, quad and trailer), machines (forklift, skid-steer loader, telehandler, wheel loader, articulated dump truck, zero-turn mower, tank) and fun ones (tuk-tuk, golf cart, ice resurfacer, shopping trolley, F1 car). They steer five ways: front, rear, all-wheel (with a crab mode on the telehandler), skid (pivots on the spot) and frame (bends in the middle). Eight games so far. **Valet Rush** parks and fetches customers' cars on the open lot against a queue, **Hot Lap** laps the F1 car round a race track, **Ferry Loader** stows a queue of vehicles on a ferry deck, **Mow Day** mows a seeded garden in stripes, **Airport** works an apron: pushback, a baggage run, a tow to the gate and a whole turnaround, **Rodeo** drives any garage vehicle through a seeded cone course of gates, a U-turn and a slalom against the clock (all below). The **Warehouse** moves pallets with a rear-steering forklift (below). **Tight Spot** is parking: spots generated from a seed (parallel at a kerb, nose in, reverse in, angled, a garage up to the wall, bays between pillars, a narrow aisle, a tight garage door, a dead-end turnaround, the end bay of a full dead-end aisle, a bus stop, a bus depot, a driveway, a slipway, a camping pitch, a loading dock and an underground garage), each with the vehicles the planner proves it with, a random spot of a picked kind (or any) with a seeded daily, a 48-level ladder, and a 3 × 3 open lot where any free bay counts, kerbside bays and underground garages included. The app was Tight Spot before the hub; its id stayed `tight-spot`, so achievements, the daily and links carried over.

Three layers, each replaceable on its own (the parking brainstorm's later steps):

| Layer | Files | Replaced by |
|-------|-------|-------------|

**The game contract.** `games/{id}/index.ts` default-exports `define_game({ id, name, icon, building, achievements, load })`: `icon` a Phosphor icon (the switcher and the doorway card show it), `building` how its building looks in the yard (`Building.CAR_PARK`, `WAREHOUSE`, `VALET_STAND`, `APRON`, `FERRY`, `GARDEN`, `ARENA`, `TRACK`, `TEST_CENTRE`), `achievements` merged into the manifest's list (ids stay unique across games; parking kept its old ids), `load` the lazy `game.svelte`. `games/index.ts` lists the games in yard order, and `UPCOMING` the names shut buildings after them carry (empty: every game gets its own open building). A game component takes `GameProps`: `settings` (the shared `DriveSettings`), `daily` (the day of the daily the app was opened with, given only to the game it names), `menu` (the switcher snippet, first in its toolbar), `play` (the window's Game Hub tracker; a game reports its daily results through `play.daily`) and `onexit` (back to the yard). It builds from the core components and keeps its own state; nothing is shared through a context. Each game stores under `app.tight-spot.{game}.*`.

**Core components.** `drive-frame.svelte` is a game's whole frame: toolbar, status bar and board (`toolbar`, `overlay` and `status` snippets), the canvas with its device-pixel resize observer, the theme palette, wheel and pinch zoom, the touch steering (wheel or buttons) and pedals, the keys from anywhere in the window, and the board focus. The game binds `size`, `palette`, `zoom` and the boxes its camera keeps clear of, and passes `engage` (a touch control was pressed; may start the round), `onpress` (the first pointer on a pedal), `onsteer`, `onkeydown` and `onrelease`. `drive-input.svelte.ts` (`DriveInput`) holds the keys, the held controls, a controller's triggers and the wheel and turns them into the model's `Input`; `gamepad.ts` reads a controller (pure: deadzones, press edges, the button-to-key map) and `rumble.ts` buzzes it; `loop.ts` (`frame_loop`) splits each animation frame into physics steps; `keys.ts` has the key maps; `gear-strip.svelte`, `speed-chip.svelte`, `round-card.svelte`, `key-hint.svelte` and `settings-panel.svelte` are the HUD and card pieces; `settings.svelte.ts` (`DriveSettings`) the settings every game shares; `obstacles.ts` the bounding-box broad phase over a scene's solids (`collide`); `scene.ts` the shapes (solids, bays, marks, surfaces).


## The hub

### Shell

`tight-spot.app.svelte` holds which view is up (`yard` or a game id), loads a game's component on first use (kept for the window), and owns the per-window state the views share: the `DriveSettings`, the yard's vehicle (a bus picked in the garage is still there after a game), and the game just left. Opening the app shows the yard with the car at the gate. The `game` prop (`yard` or any game id: `parking`, `forklift`, `f1`, `ferry`, `mower`, `airport`, `rodeo`) opens a game straight away (a value with no registered game opens the yard); `daily` opens that day's daily of the game `mode` names (any game in `DAILY_GAMES`, what Game Hub passes), else of a daily game in `game`, else parking's. `#/app/tight-spot?game=parking` and `app launch tight-spot game=parking` pass the same prop. Entering a game stores its id in `app.tight-spot.last`. While a game's chunk loads the shell shows its own toolbar (the switcher and the game's name) over "Opening …"; a chunk that fails to load (an update pruned the old assets) reports an error toast and goes back to the yard. Play time is counted by the shell (`track_play`, one Game Hub session per window), so time in the yard and in every game counts, and switching games doesn't start a new session; parking reports its daily through the `play` prop.

The switcher is the **Games** button first in every toolbar (a signpost and the word Games; below the `@md` container width only the signpost, named Games for screen readers): a system menu (`dialogs.menu`, a bottom sheet on phones) with the Yard and each game, the current one disabled and the last one played labelled "· last". Back (`window.on_back`) in a game closes its settings, then pauses a running round, then returns to the yard, where the vehicle comes out of that game's doorway facing away. Back in the yard closes the settings, then leaves the app.

## Core

### The car

`vehicle.ts`. SI units, metres and seconds. `VehicleSpec.drive` says how a vehicle steers ([ADR-088](../adrs/088-vehicle-models.md)), a tagged union: `ackermann` with `steer_axle` `front` (every car, bus and rig), `rear` (the forklift), `all` (the ladder truck) or `crab` (the telehandler); `skid` with the track the turn feels; `frame` with the joint's place. `step()` stays the one entry and dispatches on it; `travel(placement, steer, distance, spec, crab)` is the same motion without the pedals, for the planner and the guide lines. `heading` 0 is east and grows clockwise on screen (y points down), so a positive steer turns right on every drive: steered at the rear, the rear wheels point left and the tail swings out. `car.steer` is the wheel angle on Ackermann, the wheel's share (−1 to 1) on a skid steer, the joint's bend on a frame steer; `max_steer` is its limit, and the touch wheel and Wheel° read it as a share of that.

- **Front and rear steer.** The pose is the centre of the axle that doesn't steer: the rear axle's for front steer, the front axle's for rear steer. Without slip that point only moves along the heading and the vehicle turns about a point on that axle's line, so the arc and the curvature `tan(δ) / L` are the same for both; only where the body, the wheels and the bumpers sit relative to the pose changes (`axles()`, `body_ends()`).
- **All-wheel steer** (`ALL_STEER`). The rear wheels counter-steer as far as the front ones turn, so the turn centre sits level with the middle of the wheelbase: the pose is there, and the curvature is `tan(δ) / (L/2)` (`steer_base()`), half the circle of the same truck steered only in front. `max_steer` derives δ the same way with `L/2`. Both pairs are drawn at their own angles (`atan2(x·κ, 1 − y·κ)`, negative `x` behind).
- **Crab steer** (`CRAB_STEER`). An all-wheel steer with a crab mode: `Car.crab` (on only for such a vehicle, set by `set_crab`, which refuses above `park_speed` like P) turns both axles the same way, so the body slides along the wheels at `heading + steer` without turning (`crab_move`); every wheel is drawn at `steer`, and `turn_centre` is null. Off, it is an all-wheel steer. The pose is the middle in both modes, so switching doesn't move the body.
- **Skid steer** (`{ kind: "skid", track }`). Two sides driven apart: `speed` is what the outer side covers, and the wheel's share `s` slows the inner one, down to running it backwards at full lock. The body moves `distance · (1 − |s|)` along the heading and turns `2 · distance · s / track` (`skid_move`, exact for the step), so half lock turns about the inner side, which stands still, and full lock pivots on the spot as soon as there is gas, the two sides rolling opposite ways. `track` is wider than the wheels' or the tracks' (1.6 m on the bobcat's 1.32 m), the way they scrub. The pose is the middle of the contact; the mower's contact is its drive axle (`wheelbase` 0), so it pivots about the rear wheels and its deck swings round. `max_steer` is 1.
- **Frame steer** (`{ kind: "frame", pivot }`). Two frames on a joint `pivot` behind the front axle; the pose is the rear axle and `car.steer` the bend γ. Neither axle slides, so the rear frame turns at `dθ = (v·sin γ − a·dγ) / (b·cos γ + a)` (`a` the front axle to the joint, `b` the joint to the rear axle) and the front axle covers `v·cos γ + b·dθ·sin γ`: bending at a standstill swings both frames about their axles, which stay put. `frame_move` integrates that with RK4 in pieces of at most 5 cm or 1.1°, with the bend and the speed changing linearly over the step. Held bent the rear axle circles at `sin γ / (b·cos γ + a)`. `max_steer` solves for the bend at which the outer wheel of the axle that swings widest draws `turning_radius` (40° on the loader, 47° on the dump truck). `front_frame()` is the front axle as a pose, `frame_ends()` each frame's extent along its own axis (a 0.12 m gap either side of the joint), `bodies()` the two frames, `headings()` both frames' headings, `nose()` the front bumper's middle.

The hatchback (`HATCHBACK`): 4.3 m long, 1.8 m wide, 2.6 m wheelbase, 0.8 m rear overhang (0.9 m front), 1.55 m track. Its `turning_radius` is 5.3 m kerb to kerb, the circle the outer front wheel draws at full lock. `max_steer` derives the bicycle-model lock from it: the outer front wheel sits at `sqrt(R² − L²)` from the turn centre sideways, half a track out from the rear axle's centre, so `δ = atan(L / (sqrt(R² − L²) − track/2))` = 34.1°, and the rear axle's centre turns on a 3.84 m circle.

`step(car, input, dt)` is the kinematic (no-slip) bicycle model, stepped at 120 Hz or finer: each animation frame is split into equal steps of at most 1/120 s covering exactly its own time (capped at 100 ms; a frame stamped before the loop started, or a zero step, moves nothing, since `Infinity · 0` in P would make the speed NaN), since fixed steps against a 120 Hz screen land 0, 1 or 2 to a frame and the car visibly shakes:

1. The wheel moves toward the wanted angle at up to `STEER_BOOST` (1.6) × `max_steer / steer_time`, at a standstill too: `steer_time` (1.1 s for the hatchback) is a steady hold's centre to lock, and the wheel itself can go about 0.7 s, so a held key that speeds up (Controls) isn't clipped and a touch wheel drag follows with less lag. With `steer: null` (hands off) the target is straight ahead and the rate is the steady one scaled with speed up to 2 m/s, so the wheel self-centres only while rolling, like caster.
2. Pedals ramp (3/s down, 8/s up) so a key press isn't an on/off jump.
3. In D or R the car creeps: acceleration `creep_accel · (1 − v/creep_speed)`, or the throttle's `accel · throttle · (1 − v/top)`, whichever is larger. The creep is off by default: the app passes `NO_CREEP` (`creep_accel` 0) to `advance` unless the Creep setting (`app.tight-spot.creep`) is on, so the car moves only on the gas; rolling resistance and the brake take speed off without crossing zero. P holds the car still; N coasts. `shift` refuses P above 0.3 m/s.
4. The pose moves along the exact arc for the step's distance (`arc()`), curvature `tan(δ) / L`, so the rear axle never slides sideways and a full-lock circle closes on itself.

The steered wheels are drawn at their Ackermann angles (`wheels()`): a wheel `x` along from the pose and `y` across points at `atan2(x·κ, 1 − y·κ)`, which for front steer is the familiar `atan(L / (1/κ ∓ track/2))`. `atan2` matters for rear steer: a forklift's turn centre at full lock lies inside its track, so its inner rear wheel turns past 90°. The dashed guide lines are `sweep()`: the two leading corners' paths for 5 m at the current wheel angle.

Each wheel's rolled distance is in `car.roll` (metres, signed, in `wheels()` order), for the tread drawing. `roll()` adds the step's distance times the wheel's own radius over the rear axle centre's: a wheel at `(x, y)` from the rear axle's centre turns on `|κ| · hypot(x, 1/κ − y)` of it, which is `hypot(x·κ, 1 − y·κ)` and 1 driving straight. On lock the outer front wheel rolls the most and the inner rear the least; standing still nothing rolls, whatever the wheel does.

### Trailers and hitch chains

`VehicleSpec.hitches` is the hitch chain ([ADR-088](../adrs/088-vehicle-models.md)): empty for a solo vehicle, one `TrailerSpec` for a car and trailer, several for a train. Each link pivots on the body ahead of it: `hitch` is from that body's axle (the vehicle's pose for the first link, the link before's axle after that) back to the hinge, negative when the hinge sits ahead of the axle (a semi's kingpin); `axle` the hinge to the link's own axle; `drawbar` the hinge to the front of its box, negative when the box reaches past the hinge; `max_angle` how far that hinge may fold from `rest`, the hinge angle the link hangs at (0, the default, trails behind; `Math.PI` hangs ahead of the body it's hitched to, like a towbar off a pushback tug's front with the aircraft beyond it). `BOX_TRAILER`: the ball 1.0 m behind the rear axle (h), the trailer's axle 2.45 m behind the ball (L), a 1.1 m drawbar to a 2.5 × 1.6 m box, a 1.45 m track. `Car.trailers` holds one `{ heading, roll }` per link (φ from its axle toward its hinge, and its two wheels' rolled distance), absent on a solo vehicle; `create_car` puts every link at its rest (`rest_trailers()`; `chain_rests()` sums the rests down the chain).

`step()` stays the one entry: after the vehicle's arc it calls `tow()`, which integrates the chain link by link with the standard one-trailer kinematics. With ψ = φₚ − φ the link's hinge angle against the body ahead (θ, the vehicle's heading, for the first), and v and ω that body's axle speed and turn per metre s the vehicle covers (1 and κ = tan δ / L for the vehicle itself):

```
dφ/ds = (v·sin ψ − h·ω·cos ψ) / L            (for the first link: (sin ψ − h·κ·cos ψ) / L)
dA/ds = v·cos ψ + h·ω·sin ψ                    (the distance the link's axle covers: its v for the next link)
```

θ along the arc is exact (θ₀ + κs), so RK4 over the whole chain in pieces of at most 2.5 cm stays accurate even reversing, which amplifies every error. The first link's arithmetic is the one the single trailer always had, so one-trailer rigs move bit for bit as before. Forward, every ψ decays over about its L of travel; reversing, each grows at the same rate and feeds the next, so a chain diverges faster than one trailer and a long one can't be backed at all. The wheels roll the axle's distance plus or minus half a track times the turn. A step of no time changes nothing, trailers included. `trailer_poses()` gives every link's axle as a pose, `trailer_pose`, `trailer_corners`, `trailer_wheels` and `hitch_point` take the link's index, `hitch_angles()` every hinge's angle and `folds()` each one's angle from its rest. A placement without trailers has them at rest.

The kinematics hold for any hinge angle, so a link hanging ahead needs nothing of its own there: at ψ = π a straight push keeps it at π (and is unstable the way reversing a trailer is), and pulling back straightens it. What changes is everything that assumed a link trails: `folds()` feeds `jackknife()` and the planner's hitch bins, the planner's heuristic and `headings()` turn each link back by its rest, `rig_ends` of a rig with any non-zero rest is its bodies' extent at rest, and `hangs_ahead(spec)` (the last link points forward) moves the trailer's sensors, its guide lines and the lit tail lamps to the front and leaves reversing to the vehicle.

A hinge folded past its link's `max_angle` (`jackknife()` gives the first such hinge, `jackknifed()` whether any) ends the round like a contact: `hit.kind = "jackknife"`, the ring at that hinge, and the card says "Jackknifed the trailer", or on a chain "Jackknifed at the second hinge" (`hit.hinge`). Every garage trailer folds 70° at the ball, the articulated bus's turntable included, and has rest 0; the one link at rest π is Pushback's airliner (`PUSHBACK`, 75° either side of π at the nose gear), which isn't in the garage.

Tests (`vehicle.test.ts`) pin the steering: lock in `steer_time / STEER_BOOST`, a target moved at the steady rate followed without lag, `steer_boost` flat for a short hold then rising monotonically to the cap, and the caster centring at the steady rate. And the geometry: the outer front wheel's circle is 5.3 m, the rear axle stays on its 3.84 m circle for 2000 steps, it only ever moves along the heading, the outer front corner swings outside the rear wheels' path, reversing on lock retraces the same circle, plus the steering rate, creep, brake hold, P refusal and determinism; every vehicle's lock gives its own turning radius; towing forward straightens the trailer, reversing grows the hitch angle until it jackknifes, the trailer's axle never slides sideways, its wheels roll the right distances, and towing is deterministic. A three-link chain starts straight with every link a body and the tail at `rig_ends`, its first link moves exactly as a lone trailer, forward straightens every link, reversing makes the hinges diverge until one folds past its limit, no link's axle slides sideways, each hinge jackknifes at its own limit, and it is deterministic with a step of no time (or less) a no-op. A test-local tug with one link at rest π: the link is created and placed ahead with `rig_ends` reaching its far end, a straight push keeps it at rest, it jackknifes past `max_angle` either side of π, and the planner turns the rig round into a pose box with no fold past the limit on the way. Rear steer: the pose is the front axle and the rear wheels point against the turn, full lock gives the turning radius at the outer rear wheel while the front axle stays on its circle, steering right turns right with the front axle only moving along the heading, the tail swings wider than anything at the front, each wheel rolls its own arc (the outer rear the most), and it is deterministic with a step of no time a no-op.

### Grip

In `step()`, only for the fast cars, temporary until a slip model (owner: "we will rework this when we add slip"; "i dont like slow turning for normal cars. lets just do it for gt and f1"). A kinematic car has no tyres to slide, so for a spec that sets `a_lat` (m/s²) `step()` holds the steering target to `grip_lock(speed, spec, crab)`: the most wheel at which the turn's curvature stays within `a_lat / v²`. Ackermann: `atan(steer_base · a_lat / v²)`, so `tan(steer) / steer_base` is the capped curvature. Frame steer: the bend at which the axle on the joint's shorter arm, which turns tightest on `(long · cos γ + short) / sin γ`, reaches it. A skid steer and crab mode draw no such curve and keep their lock. Only the target is capped: the wheel winds down to it at its own rate as the car speeds up. A spec without `a_lat` keeps any lock at any speed. Set: the GT car 16 (full lock to about 30 km/h, about 9 % of it at 30 m/s; raised from 8 after the owner found it too locked at speed), the garage F1 car 12 (full lock to about 35 km/h), Hot Lap's race spec 25. The planner works on geometry, so no proof is affected. `top_speed(spec)` (also in `vehicle.ts`) is the steady speed the throttle holds against rolling resistance. Tests: a GT car at 30 m/s stays within its `a_lat / v²`, far from lock, and reaches full lock at 4 m/s; a hatchback keeps full lock at any speed; a skid steer keeps its lock even with `a_lat`; a frame steer's tighter axle turns at exactly `a_lat / v²`. Moved from an opt-in `with_grip` in `core/grip.ts` into `step()` ([ADR-089](../adrs/089-driving-test.md) revision).

### Drawing

`draw.ts` draws one frame onto the canvas in world metres; `read_palette` resolves the theme once per theme change.

- **Contrast on any body.** The default theme's primary (the player's paint) is as light as its foreground, so nothing mixed from the foreground shows on it. Detail on a car takes the theme's two extremes instead: `ink`, the darker of foreground and background-darkest, and `glow`, the lightest of foreground, background-lightest and cottontail (lightness read with `oklch(from … l 0 0)`). A lamp is a `glow` (or `error`) lens in an `ink` housing, so one of the two stands off any body. Glass and roof take whichever of `ink` and the plain light tone (no cottontail) is further in lightness from that paint (`against()`), mixed into it. Parked cars' paints are mixed with the background, not with transparent, so lines don't show through them.
- **Lights.** Headlights, tail lights (brighter with a red halo while braking), reverse lamps in R. In D or R the lamps throw a soft cone on the ground (a linear gradient from `glow` to clear, shorter behind), only when the ground is much darker than `glow`; on light themes it read as a grey smear.
- **Tyres.** Drawn over the body, slightly wider than real and nudged out, so the front pair's Ackermann angles read. `tyre_out(track, tyre_width, body_width)` sets how far out: `TYRE_SHOW` (8 cm), or more where a body as wide as its track would hide them, so at least `TYRE_POKE` (11 cm) shows past the side. Every car, rig, machine and trailer goes through it (a machine that keeps its wheels under it passes 0), so the ladder truck, the box truck, the semi-trailers and the caravan show theirs. Tread stripes every 11 cm come from `tread(roll, pitch, length)`: seen from above it's the top of the tyre, which moves the way the car does, so stripes run forward in D and back in R. ### Collision

`collision.ts`, hand-written. Every body is tested: the vehicle and, towing, each trailer's box down the chain (`bodies()`); a drawbar isn't a body. `overlaps` is the separating-axis test for two convex polygons: they're apart if some edge normal of either one splits their projections. Touching counts as apart, so a car whose side lies on a wall's face isn't a crash. Every solid is convex (a rectangle or a rotated rectangle), so nothing is decomposed. `core/obstacles.ts` keeps each scene's solids with their bounding boxes (`obstacles`, cached per world) and runs SAT only on boxes that meet the car's (`collide`).

`contact_point` is where the red ring goes: the average of the corners of each shape inside the other and the edge crossings.

### Sensors

`sensors.ts` (in Tight Spot, Valet Rush, the yard, Ferry Loader and Rodeo; the Warehouse, Airport, Mow Day and Hot Lap have none): for the bumper the gear drives toward, each solid's edges are taken into the car's frame, clipped to the strip the car would sweep driving straight (half a width plus 5 cm each side) and to 1.6 m ahead; the nearest point is the reading. Towing, the rear sensor is the last trailer's (`sensor_frame`): its frame, its width and its tail (1.15 m behind its axle on the box trailer), and the arcs are drawn there. `sound.ts` beeps a 1750 Hz tone, quicker the closer (`beep_period`), steady under 30 cm, from an `AudioContext` made on first use. The readout in the corner and the arcs at the bumper go from warning to error under 50 cm. The same `Sounds` plays the other cues, all behind the Sound setting: a quiet tick on a gear change, a falling thunk on a touch, two rising notes when parked and a third for a new best.

### Controls

Keyboard: arrows or WASD turn the wheel and work the pedals by direction. The wheel turns at the spec's steady rate (`steer_time`). Only winding it back from the other side speeds up: held while the wheel still points the opposite way, it eases up to `STEER_BOOST` times that (`steer_boost(held, against)`: 1 until 0.25 s, then a smoothstep to 1.6 over 0.3 s), and drops back to the steady rate once the wheel passes centre, so turning toward a side is always the fine rate. Letting go or flipping the direction starts over. The touch ◀ ▶ buttons run through the same `step_wheel`, so they ramp the same way. Pedals: ↑/W is gas in D (and P or N) and the brake in R, ↓/S the other way round; a fresh press (not a key repeat) while the car stands below 0.05 m/s shifts to that direction (`choose_direction`), so braking to a stop and pressing again backs up; E, R or Q, N, P or Space pick gears; C centres the wheel; V switches crab mode on a vehicle that has one (`CRAB_KEY`); Z and X are the turn signals in a game that judges them (`SIGNAL_KEYS`, Signals below); + and − zoom. Escape pauses and resumes; Enter starts, resumes, parks when the car stands in a bay it would count in (the same check P runs, so it never shifts to P outside one), and after a round runs the card's main button (`primary_action`); R tries again after a round. The keys are read on the app's root, not only the board, so a click on a toolbar button or a HUD gear doesn't strand them (fields, the Segmented radios and the open settings panel keep theirs). Closing the settings hands the focus back to the board, and a key that lands on the page body (after the guide closes) is routed to the board while the window is in front. The setting "Keyboard wheel" picks between self-centring as the car rolls (default) and staying put; on touch it shows only in Buttons mode, as "Steering buttons".

Touch (`pointer: coarse`): steering is a wheel you drag round by the rim (`steering-wheel.svelte`, about 1.25 turns each way, held where you leave it, the hub centres it, movement near the hub ignored so a slipping thumb doesn't spin it), or, with the Steering setting on Buttons (`app.tight-spot.steering`), two held ◀ ▶ buttons that turn the wheel at the keyboard's rate and follow the same Centres/Stays setting as the arrow keys (shown on touch only in Buttons mode). Held Gas and Brake pedals (pointer capture, released on `lostpointercapture`, lit while held) work like ↑ and ↓: Gas means forward and Brake back, so the one against the gear brakes, and a fresh press while the car stands (P and N included) shifts that way through the same `choose_direction` as the keys. Brake from P at the start reverses. The one gear control is the P R N D strip on the board's top edge, 44 px targets on touch; the speed then stacks over the sensor readout at the top right so the strip has room. Pinch to zoom. Each control takes its own pointer, so steering and the pedals work at once.

**Controller.** `drive-frame.svelte` polls the first `standard`-mapping pad (`navigator.getGamepads()`, `first_pad`) once a frame while one is connected (from `gamepadconnected`, or found at mount), and only in the window in front with the page visible and the browser window focused (`document.hasFocus()`, like `rumble()`), so two browser windows side by side don't both drive from one pad; a window in the back reads it as released. Losing the pad or the front while the stick is deflected lets go of the wheel the same way releasing the stick does. Nothing polls without a pad. Mapping (racing layout):

| Pad | Does | How it reaches the game |
|-----|------|-------------------------|
| Left stick across | Sets the wheel's position: full deflection is full lock. Radial deadzone 0.2, rescaled so it starts from 0 at its edge. Let go, it lets go of the wheel like the arrows do: with the wheel setting on Centres it self-centres as the car rolls, on Stays it stays | `onsteer` (the touch wheel's path) while deflected and the wheel isn't already there; `DriveInput.let_go()` on release |
| RT / LT | Gas / brake by direction, analog 0..1 (deadzone 0.05): RT means forward, LT back, so against the gear it brakes. Past 0.15 counts as a press: a fresh one from standing shifts that way (`engage` then `onpress`, exactly like a touch pedal) | `DriveInput.set_pad(gas, brake)`, merged with keys and pedals by taking the larger |
| A / B / Y | Drive / Reverse / Neutral | `onkeydown` with `KeyE` / `KeyR` / `KeyN` |
| X | Crab | `KeyV` (`CRAB_KEY`) |
| D-pad ← / → | Turn signal left / right | `KeyZ` / `KeyX` (`SIGNAL_KEYS`); a game that doesn't judge signals ignores them |
| Menu | The primary action key | `Space`: Park in parking and the yard (starts a round from its start card), the forks in the warehouse |
| RB | Enter | `Enter`: the primary action everywhere: start or resume a round, park in a bay, a card's main button (Next, New spot), go into a building from the yard |
| View | Pause | `Escape`: pauses and resumes a round. No game has a mid-round restart, and R mid-round is Reverse, so after a round the card's retry stays on the keyboard (R), RB and touch |

Buttons arrive on a press edge (`pressed()`) as a synthetic `KeyboardEvent` with the key's `code` (`key_event`), so every game handles them in its own `on_keydown`, with no game code for the pad. RB sends Enter, so a card's main button and a building's doorway work from the pad; other card buttons and menus stay mouse, touch and keyboard. A pad that connects while the window is in front shows "Controller connected" on the board for 2.5 s. The key hints stay keys.

Rumble (`vibrationActuator.playEffect("dual-rumble")`, Chrome and Safari): `Sounds.crash()` sends a strong 220 ms pulse, and the parking sensor a light 40 ms tick with each beep faster than 0.3 s apart and every 0.12 s while the tone is steady. `Sounds` takes the window's settings (`new Sounds(() => settings)`): Sound gates the audio and Rumble the pad, each on its own, so a muted game still rumbles. The sensor's ticks are paced on `performance.now()`, not the audio clock, for the same reason. `rumble()` buzzes the first standard pad while the page has focus; no module state.

A phone held sideways (`pointer: coarse` and the app's own `ts-app` size container wider than 1.25 × its height and under 30rem tall) folds the status bar away and tightens the toolbar to 4 px of padding, so the board gets about 30 px more of its ~300; the time and moves show as a chip next to the gear strip instead. Pure CSS: `@container ts-app` inside `@media (pointer: coarse)`.

**Per drive.** The same wheel, keys and pedals drive every kind; what the wheel means changes. On a skid steer it turns (full lock spins on the spot once you give it gas), on a frame steer it bends the joint (at a standstill too), on an all-wheel steer it steers both axles. A crab-capable vehicle has a Crab chip next to the gear strip (`crab-toggle.svelte`, 44 px on touch, pressed while on), and V (X until the turn signals took it); it switches only near a standstill. The start card and the garage card show the steering key for the drive (`steer_hints`: turn, bend, steer, and V crab) and a line on how it drives (`drive_note`), on touch too.

**Signals.** `signal.ts`, decided in [ADR-089](../adrs/089-driving-test.md) for the Driving Test. `Input.signal` (`left`, `right`, `none`; optional, `step` ignores it) is the turn signal. `DriveInput` keeps the stalk (`signal`, rune state for the HUD and buttons) and reads it into every `Input`: `toggle_signal(side)` switches it on, the same side again cancels it, the other side switches over. It cancels itself like a real stalk (`signal_after`, pure): once the wheel has turned more than 0.3 of lock toward the signalled side, bringing it back under 0.05 of lock throws it off; a turn the other way (steering left round a roundabout with the right signal on) and small corrections leave it on. `step_wheel` and `follow` both run the check, so it works with hands on the keys, the touch wheel, a stick, or hands off while the wheel self-centres. Keys Z (left) and X (right), which is why crab moved to V; the pad's D-pad ← → (standard buttons 14 and 15) send the same keys. On touch `drive-frame.svelte` puts two 44 px buttons above the wheel when the game passes `signals` (off by default), lit and blinking with the blinkers (`step-end` 0.8 s, steady with reduced motion). `Sounds.indicator(on)`, called every frame, clicks the relay twice a period (`BLINK_PERIOD` 0.8 s), behind the Sound setting. `player(…, time, signal)` draws lit blinkers at the signalled side's front and rear corners of any vehicle (`blinkers`: the rig's outline in front, the last link's box at the back of a chain), the `warning` token over a `warning_glow` halo, for the first half of each period (`blink_lit(time)`). Every game gets the key handling for free but none of it shows unless the game handles the keys, passes `signals` and the signal to `player`: only the Driving Test does.

Upright, the controls sit in a row under the board: steering left, pedals right. When the stage is wider than 1.25 × its height (a phone held sideways) a container query lets the board fill the whole stage and floats the steering over its left edge and the pedals over its right, near the bottom where the thumbs rest, on translucent backgrounds mixed from the theme's background and foreground. The steering hides while a round card is up (the card sits on the left there), the pedals while paused. Back (`window.on_back`) closes the settings, then pauses a running round, then returns to the yard.

The camera follows the car with a little look-ahead, framed in the part of the board left clear by the top chips (always), the end card once a round is over (bottom, or left on a wide board) and the sideways touch controls (`frame` takes insets: the focus sits in the middle of the clear part and the world's edge may slide under a covered strip, so the car never ends up under a thumb at the edge of the map). At zoom 1 it shows 26 m across the board's shorter side for the hatchback, zoomed by vehicle length both ways (`view_span`: 26 m × `view_stretch`, the rig's length over 4.3 m clamped to 0.6–1.6×), so a bus fills about the share of the screen a hatchback does without bay lines shrinking to hairlines on a phone, and a tuk-tuk or a golf cart isn't a speck without the lot getting too close to read. Before the start it frames the car and its bay together (the whole of both when a long rig or a long bay reaches past the usual margin, like a road train and its dock lane), clear of the start card and, held sideways, of the pedals; an arrow on the edge points to an off-screen bay. The toolbar's Play button starts, pauses and resumes; below `@md` it moves onto the board's top-left corner, next to the gear strip and speed, which only show once a round has started.

Round cards sit at the bottom of a tall board and on the left, centred, of a board wider than 1.2 × its height (a desktop window, a phone held sideways; `side_card`) so the bay, the car and the contact ring stay visible. The start card has the kind's brief, the vehicle as a badge, its par and the best result with the best precision, the kind picker below `@md` and the vehicle picker below `@2xl`, and the keys on a fine pointer; the parked card reveals its stars one by one, shows the precision with its three offsets, and marks a Perfect park, a first park or a new best; the crashed card names what was touched, or says the trailer jackknifed. ## Tight Spot (parking)

`games/parking/`: the round model (`game.ts`), the world and lots, the frame (`draw.ts`), the component (`game.svelte`), the tests.

### World: chunks and plans

`core/lot.ts`, shared by every game that drives a street of lots (Parking and Valet Rush). The world is a grid of 32 m chunks. `chunk(seed, cx, cy, plan)` is pure: the same seed, cell and plan give the same chunk. A `Plan` says what each cell is in a mode (a road, a kerbside road, a lot facing some side with the `build(rng)` that lays it out, or outside the world); the chunk reads its neighbours through it to open exits:

- road to road is always open (a kerbside road counts as a road); road to lot only through the lot's `entry`; lot to lot never;
- a side facing the outside of the world gets a wall.

Road chunks are an 8 m carriageway: a centre square plus an arm per open exit (a piece with one exit runs straight on to the far edge), kerbs elsewhere. A **kerbside** chunk (`ChunkKind.KERBSIDE`, its cell carrying the `spec` to size for) is the same road with a lay-by let into the kerb on both sides of each east–west arm (`lay_bys`): a parallel bay `Fit.length + 2.1 m × Fit.turn` long (the parallel spot's gap at the open lot's difficulty) and 2.5 m plus the extra width deep, with 1.5 m sloped ends where the arm has room (less when the bay is long), so the carriageway keeps both lanes clear. Each lay-by is a free bay facing east or west, `either`, or a parked car (a third of them), from the chunk's own random stream. The kerb corners it cuts become six convex pieces. A trailer, or a bay longer than the 11 m arm, gets no lay-by. Lots are laid out in their own frame (a cell's `build`, from `core/lot-layout.ts` or parking's `lots.ts`), entry through the south edge between x 12 and 20, then turned in quarter turns to face their entry. `chunk_rng` seeds each chunk from `hash(seed, cx, cy)` with `core/utils/random.ts`.

Plans today:

| Mode | Plan |

| Spot, level, daily | `scenario_world` (`games/parking/world.ts`, building the kind with `build_lot`): the lot at (0, 0) facing south, a straight road at (0, 1); the car waits on the road facing north in the right lane, further up for a rig whose tail would reach the barrier at the road's end (the articulated bus), unless the lot names its own `start` |

### Every spot can be reached

`planner.ts` is a hybrid A*: nodes keep exact poses, the closed set is a grid of 0.25 m, 5°, each hinge's angle in 11° steps (none without a trailer; a chain's key is a string, since its further hinges would overflow a safe integer) and direction. A move (`Move`: `steer`, the car's own steer value, `distance` and an optional `crab`) holds the wheel for 0.6 m, forwards and backwards, at five steering shares up to full lock; a move that runs into something, or jackknifes, stops short at the last clear 0.15 m, the way a driver creeps up to a bumper. Each 0.15 m piece runs through `drive()`, which is `travel()`: the same `arc()` and `tow()` as `step()`, so every link's angle is integrated along every path, reversing included. Per drive: a skid steer has nine shares (±1, ±0.5, ±0.25, ±0.1, 0), so full lock is a pivot on the spot (0.6 m of track is 49° for the bobcat) and the small ones are gentle curves; a crab-capable vehicle adds four crab moves (±0.5 and ±1 of lock); a frame steer's move is a bend toward the share at `FRAME_RAMP` (0.5 rad per metre rolled, about 3 m lock to lock), so the bend is continuous along a path and part of the node, and the closed set bins it in place of a hitch, in 22.5° steps (11° split the set so finely that a dump truck took 20–30 s a seed). Front, rear and all-wheel steer keep their five arcs. Cost is distance (reverse × 1.3) plus 2.5 per change of direction, heuristic the straight distance plus 3 m per radian of heading error (the vehicle's, plus each trailer's), weighted 4. `inflate` grows every link's box. The goal is within `reach` and `heading_tolerance` of `to`, or any test the caller passes as `arrive`.

`proof.ts` (test-only, like `poses.ts`) proves a spot two ways, both with the bodies inflated by 5 cm:

- **From the start.** The search starts from the car exactly as `create_game` places it, a trailer straight behind, aims at a parked pose (`parked_poses`: offsets inside the bay that count as parked and keep 5 cm clear) and stops at the first placement `parked_in` accepts. The test then drives the moves again from that start in the same 15 cm pieces with the real bodies and asserts no contact, no jackknife and `verdict()` "inside". That's what a player can do, the unstable trailer reverse included. Trailer kinds and all 48 levels go this way.
- **Out of the bay.** From a parked pose to within 1.5 m and 30° of the start; the model has no slip, so the same path backwards gets in from there. It's cheaper, and only sound without a trailer (reversing one from a hitch angle a hair off the path's folds away), so it's used for the seed sweeps of car and bus kinds and the solo machines. A frame steer's way out must also end with the joint straight, as the round starts: bending at a standstill swings both frames, so a path ending bent would start the reverse with a bend no step checked.

A towed rig at the bus stop goes in forwards and pulls up straight, so its proof searches forward only and arrives once the front section is within 3° of the bay and driving straight ahead from there (up to 20 m, in 15 cm pieces, clear of everything) ends with the game calling it parked (`pull_up`); the straight run is added to the moves before they're driven again from the start. A forward-only search alone can't line both sections up (its 11° hitch bins merge the states that would), and with reversing allowed the 18 m bus took 10–30 s a seed from the road and failed some. At the depot the articulated bus gets the plain search from the start, 1–10 s a seed.

`planner.test.ts` sweeps every kind with each vehicle its levels pick from (`ladder`, or all of them for the camping pitch) at difficulty 0, 0.5 and 1 over 8 seeds, and every level. The vehicles a kind gained since (`added()`: a look on a spec already proven there is skipped) are swept beside it in two files so they run in parallel: `planner-vehicles.test.ts` the solo ones over 8 seeds, `planner-rigs.test.ts` the towing rigs over 2, each seed a search from the start with the trailer. Per seed on the owner's machine (2 seeds a test, the worst difficulty): the slipway boat about 5 s (10.3 s at difficulty 0), the driveway horse box about 4 s (7.3 s at 0; 15.9 s once under load from another run), the quad's twitchy little trailer about 4 s (8.3 s at 1), the articulated bus at the depot about 7 s (13.8 s at 0.5) and at the bus stop under 1 s (1.5 s at 0). The machines are in `planner-vehicles.test.ts` over 8 seeds, out of the bay like the cars (a skid, crab or frame path is as reversible as an arc): the dump truck is the slowest, 11–20 s a test at the depot and the loading dock while the other files run (about 1–3 s a seed alone), the bobcat and the mower in the angled bays 4–6 s, the rest under 5 s. ### Judging

`game.ts`. Any contact ends the round (`advance` returns `crashed` with the contact point and what was hit). Towing, a hinge folded past its limit ends it too. Shifting into P checks `bay_for`: parked when every corner of every body (the car, and the trailer's box) is inside the bay polygon and the car's heading, the trailer's and a frame steer's front frame's (`headings()`), are within 5° of the bay's (or of its reverse when `either`). P anywhere else just stops the car, and a line at the bottom of the board says "Not inside a bay", "Not straight in the bay" or "Facing the wrong way" (within 5° of the reverse of a bay that isn't `either`) (`verdict`), but only once the car has been in gear, since a round starts in P.

Moves are changes between D and R, the first gear and N or P not counted. Stars: at or under the kind's par is three, two more is two, else one (pars: parallel 2, nose in 0, reverse 1, angled 0, garage 0, pillars 1, narrow 2, door 2, dead end 2, end bay 2, bus stop 1, depot 1, driveway 2, slipway 2, camping pitch 2, loading dock 2, underground 1; open lot 1). Time is the sim's own clock (`ms`), so pauses and a hidden window don't count.

#### Precision

`precision(car, bay, spec)`, a score apart from the stars, which stay moves only. From every corner of every body (the car and, towing, the trailer's box) a ray runs to the bay's outline along the bay's axis, back, and across both ways; the shortest of each is that side's gap, so a crooked car or a trailer folded to one side shows in the gap it closes. The axis is the bay's heading, or its reverse when an `either` bay is entered the other way round.

- side offset `s = (right − left) / 2`, out of `(right + left) / 2`, the room either side when centred;
- depth offset `d = (front − rear) / 2`, out of `(front + rear) / 2`;
- angle `a`, the worse of the car's, the trailer's and a frame steer's front frame's heading against the axis, out of the 5° that still counts as parked.

Each error is its share `e` of that room, clamped to 0–1, and the score is `100 × (1 − e_s²)(1 − e_d²)(1 − e_a²)`: 100 dead centre and square, 0 against a line or 5° off, and falling as any one grows. A trailer rig is judged as one footprint, not by whichever body seems to matter: in a driveway, on a slipway or on a camping pitch both are in the bay and both have to be placed. Squaring the shares keeps small slips cheap (6 cm off in a bay with 45 cm either side is 98 %) and the edges expensive. The parked card shows "Precision N %" with ↔ the side offset, ↕ the depth offset (cm) and ∠ the angle; 95 % or more (rounded) adds a Perfect badge next to the stars and unlocks the Perfect park achievement. The bays are sized differently, so the same centimetres score differently by kind: a parallel gap or a bus stop box has metres of depth to spare, a garage with the shelf very little.


## Warehouse (forklift)

`games/forklift/`: the floor and job generator (`warehouse.ts`), the round model and its rules (`game.ts`), the job planner and par (`plan.ts`, `par.worker.ts`, `par.ts`), the frame (`draw.ts`), the component (`game.svelte`), the proofs (`prove.ts` and the `planner-*.test.ts` files). Decided in [ADR-087](../adrs/087-driving-hub.md) and its brainstorm's "Forklift details".

### The floor

`build_warehouse(seed, difficulty)`, pure. A walled floor; along the north wall a single rack row opening south (face A); one or two back-to-back rows below it (faces B and C, D and E), each face a row of 6–8 bays between 0.1 m uprights with a back rail; aisles between the rows; cross aisles at both ends with a 0.5 m pillar at each double row's end; a main aisle; three to six dock spots along the south wall, each in front of a dock door (Dock A…); the charging bay in the south-east corner, where the forklift starts facing north. `layout()` sets the sizes from the difficulty (all `lerp`s) and the seed:

| | Roomy (0) | Tight (1) |
|---|---|---|
| Aisle | 4.6 m (+0–0.25) | 4.4 m (+0–0.25) |
| Room either side of a pallet in a bay | 0.2 m | 0.1 m |
| Main aisle | 5.6 m (+0–0.3) | 4.8 m (+0–0.3) |
| Double rows | one (75 % of seeds below 0.3) | two |
| Stocked bays | 35 % | 70 % |
| Faces with three tiers | 35 % | 80 % |
| Job's rack slots on an upper tier | 25 % | 60 % |

A pallet is 1.2 m along the forks by 1.0 m. A rack bay is 1.25 m deep to its back rail; its **slot** (the area a pallet counts as put in) runs from 0.2 m out in the aisle to 2 cm short of the rail, between the uprights, and a stored pallet sticks out 8 cm past the front line so the forklift's body stays clear of the uprights while its forks are in. A dock spot is the pallet with 0.3 m all round. `Slot.heading` is the way the forks point going in; `Slot.home` is where a stored pallet sits.

**Tiers.** Every rack face has two or three tiers (`Face.tiers`): the floor, a beam at 1.5 m and one at 3 m (`TIER_HEIGHTS`). A bay's tiers share its footprint, so `slots` holds one `Slot` per bay per tier, each with `tier` (0 the floor, docks always 0) and `tiers` (its bay's count); `slot_label` names one "B4" or "B4 ↑2". The tightest aisle is 4.4 m (it was 4.0 m before the tiers): standing square at a rack face with the fork tips, or the load, short of an upper pallet needs the whole forklift in the aisle.

`build_floor(seed, difficulty, size)` adds the job: for each of `size` pallets a task (pick from a rack bay to a dock spot, put away from a dock spot to a bay, or move between faces), every bay used once, then stock in the other bays' floor slots. Then, from a rng of its own (so the floor-level choices of every seed stay what they were before the tiers), each of the job's rack slots moves to an upper tier with `upper_share(difficulty)`, and the other slots of every tier fill with stock at the same density. Stock is `stock: Slot[]`; the fixed solids (walls, pillars, uprights, rails) are `solids`, and the stock of each tier its own scene in `shelves[tier]`, so the forks and the load only meet the tier they're at. The job's pallets are not solids, since they move.

### Picking up and putting down

- **Forks in a pallet.** `fork_fit` measures the forklift against a pallet: the angle off its axis, how far its centre is off the forks' centre line, and the gap from the mast to its face. The forks are **in its pockets** within `JAM_ANGLE` 10° of its axis and with its centre at most `JAM_SIDE` 15 cm off the forks' centre line. That's looser than the fork spread allows (`FORK_ROOM` = (pallet width − fork spread) / 2 = 7 cm), on purpose: real forks slide under a pallet a little off, and the game forgives it (owner, 2026-10-29: the old 5° and 7 cm were too sensitive). Outside them, forks overlapping a pallet jam (a bump, below). Precision still rewards square: the drop is scored against the target as before, and the target still wants the load within 5°.
- **Lift** (`fork_action` → `lift`): standing (under 0.05 m/s), forks in the pockets (square within 5° by that rule) and fully in (mast within 15 cm of the face, `FULL_GAP`). The pallet then rides at the offset it was picked up with (`Load.carried`, the pallet's pose in the forklift's frame), and joins the bodies that collide.
- **Lower:** standing, the carried pallet's every corner inside the task's target slot and its heading within 5° of the slot's. It stays where it was put; the next task starts.
- **Contact** (`contact()`, the same function the planner uses with a 5 cm margin; play passes 0): the body against the fixed solids, the floor's stock and any job pallet; the forks against the fixed solids, the stock of their tier and the job pallets on it outside their pockets (`JAM`); then the carried pallet against the same (`by_load`). The forks' tier (`fork_tier`) is the floor, or `GameState.raised`'s tier while the forks or the load still reach into that slot (`settle_raised` drops it once they don't), so at travel height they pass under an upper pallet and meet only the uprights and the floor's stock; `near_of(floor)` caches the obstacles per floor. The drawing shifts an upper pallet 9 cm up and left per tier so a stacked bay reads; collision always uses the real pose.
- **Nothing fails: a contact is a bump** (owner's call, wave 3 brainstorm, "there is always bumps everywhere"). `advance` undoes the step (`bump()`): the forklift and any load stay where they were (the carried pallet keeps its offset), speed 0, gear kept, and the round goes on. The first bump against something adds `BUMP_COST_MS` 5 s to the clock and counts in `bumps`; `GameState.bumped` holds what was hit and where the forklift stood, and pressing on is the same bump until it has backed off `BUMP_CLEAR` 0.3 m (Valet's dent rule). The contact rings flash for `BUMP_FLASH_MS` 1.2 s (`bump_flash`), a bump sounds, and `bump_hint` names it over `fork_hint` while `bumped` is set: "Bumped a rack: back off", "The load bumped a pallet: back off", "Jammed: back out". Pressing on, a step that ends just short is taken, so the forklift creeps up to the thing and stops. The done card lists the bumps and their seconds; the time star is judged on the clock with them in. There is no crashed state. The planner and `prove()` still treat any contact as blocked (`contact() !== null`), so the proofs are unchanged.
- **End stops** (`end_stop`). Two pallet contacts are where the job wants the forklift to stop, so they stop it (speed 0, the step undone) with no bump and no cost: the mast against the pallet in hand with the forks in its pockets and fully in (gap within `FULL_GAP`), and, lined up at the face of the current upper pick or put-down (`at_pick_face` / `at_drop_face` the step before), the forks or load at travel height against the stock on the floor under that slot, which the pallet drawn above hides. The second was the owner's "too sensitive" report (2026-10-29): forks at B7's face bumped the floor stock under B7 ↑1.
- `fork_hint` says why the Fork control is off once the forklift stands at the pallet: in the pockets "Drive the forks fully in"; short of it (tips not in yet, within 25° and 50 cm) and off the pockets "Line up: 12.5° off" or "Line up: pallet 20 cm to the left", before the tines go in and jam; or carries one within 2.2 m of the target ("Not straight…", "Not inside Dock C yet"); `fork_depth` is the HUD's *Forks N %*.

### Upper tiers and the side view

`rack.ts`, decided in [the rack tiers brainstorm](../brainstorm/2026-10-29_forklift-rack-tiers.md) (idea from LaxDOTexe). Floor picks and drops stay as above; the side view opens for upper tiers only.

- **At the face.** With the pallet in hand on an upper tier, `fork_action` says `rack` (the Fork control reads Raise) once the forklift stands with the forks in line with its pockets (`in_pockets`) and the tips at most `STANDOFF` = 1 m short of its face, not past it (`at_pick_face`). Carrying to an upper tier, it's the load square to the slot, its front 0–1 m short of the face, and a straight run in would put it inside (`at_drop_face`). `fork_hint` says "Stop at the rack face: it's up on ↑2", "Back out to the rack face: …" or "Not lined up with B4". `fork_depth` (Forks N %) is off for upper pallets.
- **The side view** (`open_side`, `GameState.side`): the forks start at travel height (`TRAVEL` 10 cm). Up and down (↑/↓, W/S, the Gas and Brake pedals, a controller's triggers, the panel's buttons) raise and lower them at `LIFT_RATE` 0.4 m/s up to `MAST` 3.6 m; right and left (→/←, D/A, the steer buttons, a controller's stick pushed past halfway, `SIDE_STICK`, the panel's buttons) creep the forklift straight in and out at `CREEP` 0.3 m/s, never back past where the view opened nor further in than fully in. The height holds while the tips (or the load) are in. `step_side` runs instead of `advance` while the view is open; the clock keeps running.
- **The height.** The ideal is the tines dead centre in the pockets, `POCKET` 5 cm over the beam, for a pick; for a put-down the load's underside `CLEARANCE` 5 cm over the beam as well (`ideal_lift`). It's judged the moment the tips or the load pass the face (`judge`): within `CLEAN` 4 cm it goes in clean, up to `MISS` 10 cm it scrapes (allowed, it costs precision), beyond that it's a bump that stops the forks short of the face: too low the beam ("The forks hit the beam: raise the forks", or the load), too high on a pick the pallet ("Jammed: lower the forks"). The rings flash on the tips in the panel and the hint shows in it; pushing in with the forks still moving keeps their new height, so holding raise and in together rises through the bump and goes in. The bump clears once the forks are at a height that goes in, or the forklift creeps back `BUMP_CLEAR` or as far as the view lets it (back to where it opened, for a bump closer than that); a height bump also goes when the view closes or opens, so its hint doesn't outlive the view and the next wrong-height push is a new bump. The uprights still collide through `contact()`, raised into the slot.
- **Lift and put down.** F (or the Fork control) lifts once the forks are fully in (`can_lift`), or puts the load down once it's inside the slot (`placed_in`); `use_side` returns to the floor with the forks still raised into the slot (`raised`), so backing out over the stock below is clean, then they go down to travel. Esc or Back (the phone's too, before it pauses) leaves the view with the forks out.
- **The panel** (`draw-side.ts`, over the board under the HUD, clear of the wheel and pedals when they sit beside the board on a phone held sideways, through the camera's `control_insets()`): the forklift's front, mast, carriage and tines at their height, the bay's uprights and beams with the pallets on its tiers, the clean (success) and scrape (warning) bands round the ideal height, a readout ("Forks 1.53 m", "Pockets ↑1: 3 cm low" in the verdict's colour) and a gauge of the whole mast down the right edge; under it four 44 px hold buttons (lower, raise, out, in), Back and, on a fine pointer, the Fork button and key hints.
- **Top shelf** (achievement): a put-down on the highest tier of its bay (↑1 on a two-tier face, ↑2 on a three-tier one) that goes in clean.
- **From above** an upper tier's pallet is drawn shifted up and left per tier, so a stacked bay reads; the job's pills carry the tier ("1 ↑2", "→1 ↑2") and so does the task line ("pick up at B4 ↑2").

Tasks go in order; the HUD names the one in hand (`task_line`: "Pallet 2/3 · pick up at B4", then "Pallet 2/3 → Dock C"), the current pallet pulses, its number in a pill in the aisle at its open face (on the pallet when zoomed far out, hidden while the forks are in it), and the current task's target is outlined in the primary colour with a glow (pulsing while its pallet is on the forks) and a filled "→N" pill; later targets are dashed with an outlined "→N", done ones green; and the core's off-screen pointer leads to the pallet or, carrying, to the target.

### Scoring

- Stars (`stars`): one for the job done, one for moves (D↔R changes) at or under `par_moves` = 5 per pallet less 2 (in, out, in, out per pallet, the last needs no backing out, plus one spare each), one for the time, bumps' 5 s each included, at or under the par time.
- **Par time** (`par_of`): the planner drives the whole job from the real start (below); its path length at 1.4 m/s, plus 2.5 s per change of direction 2 s per lift or lower and 10 s more per lift or put-down on an upper tier (`PAR_RACK`, counted by `racked`), times 1.25 for slack, rounded up to 5 s. The planner's own changes of direction aren't the moves par: its fine steering shuffles more than a driver does. It runs in a worker per round (`work_out_par`), one to six seconds for four pallets, started once per round: the component keys it on `$derived(game.round)`, whose object only changes with a new round (Again reuses it, so the par is kept). A job is recorded the moment it's done, with the star for the job, the moves star and the time star when the par is already in; a job that ends first keeps waiting for its round's par (its worker isn't cancelled by Again, New or a level change) and is recorded again with the time star when it arrives and earns it. A planner that finds nothing (ruled out for every level and the sampled jobs) leaves the time star unearned.
- **Precision** per drop (`drop_fit`): `fit()` from `core/precision.ts` with the pallet's corners and heading against the target slot, as parking scores a car in a bay, times 1 − e² for the height miss of its lift and of its put-down on an upper tier (e = miss / 10 cm, so 2 cm costs 4 %, 5 cm a quarter); the card shows the average (95 % or more is Perfect) and each drop.
- Best: more stars, then time (`record_best`).
- Levels (`level_round`): 16, four each of one to four pallets, difficulty 0 → 1, seeds `hash(0xf02c, index)`. Jobs (`job_round`): the picked size, difficulty 0.15–0.85 from the seed. Daily (`daily_round`): three pallets, difficulty 0.3–0.8, seed `daily_seed(day, "tight-spot/forklift")`.

### Every job can be done

`plan_job` chains hybrid A* legs from the forklift's start: to each pallet (forks fully and squarely in; the pick leg counts as lined up only with the tips still short of the pallet, 1° inside 5° and 2 cm inside the fork spread's 7 cm, `FORK_ROOM`, tighter than the forgiving pockets, so the straight run in never leans on their edge and the pars stayed as they were), then with it to its target, every other pallet where the job has left it, the bodies grown by 5 cm through the same `contact()`. Each leg searches only until the forklift is lined up with the slot (within 5°, in the pockets or the target's side room, the slot ahead) and a straight run in reaches the goal; the run is appended (`straight_in`), so the search never has to hit a centimetre window. At an upper tier the straight run in is the side view's: it's checked with the forks raised into the slot, the lined-up pose has to stand short of the face, and after the lift or the put-down the next leg starts raised into that slot. The search itself treats an upper pallet, and an upper target's floor slot, as if they stood on the floor (wandering in under them only cost it its expansions), and tries a stage at the face before one 2.4 m back, since the window to stand square at an upper face is narrow. The planner gets the game's collision through `PlanOptions.blocked`, finer steering (nine shares of lock, `steering`) and a higher cost per change of direction (`switch_cost` 8), which halved its shuffles. `prove()` drives every leg again in 15 cm pieces with the real bodies and no margin and checks the lift at each pallet and the drop at each target, the forks lowered for good once out of a raised slot as in a round. The proofs: all 16 levels (`planner-levels.test.ts`, `planner-levels-late.test.ts`, side by side, 0.5–13 s a level) and two random jobs of each size plus a fixed daily (`planner-jobs.test.ts`).

`warehouse.test.ts`: the same seed builds the same floor, difficulty tightens it, every slot's pallet fits it and touches nothing, the start is clear, a job uses distinct slots with free targets, two or three tiers a face and more of the job up high with difficulty. `game.test.ts`: the fork rules and the forgiving pockets, a carried pallet's offset, a whole one-pallet job through lift and lower with its precision, a jam as a bump (stopped where it was, pressing on one bump, backing off clearing it, a second one counted), the mast against the pallet in hand as an end stop, a body bump costing 5 s and naming what it hit, moves, stars, bests, levels; at an upper tier the face and its hint, the end stop at the face on the floor stock under it, the lift rate and the held height, clean and scraped lifts, the raised forks coming down once out, a height miss either way stopping the forks short until it's fixed, raise and in held together rising through it, a height bump dropped with the view, a bump near the view's start clearing back there, leaving the view, the height's cost in precision.

Played through in a browser from the arrow keys and F (the planner's level 1 turned into key presses by a closed-loop driver over `DriveInput` and `advance`, replayed under Playwright's fake clock): the pick, the carry and the drop, the Fork button lighting up, the done card and the achievement.

## Hot Lap (f1)

`games/f1/`: the tracks (`track.ts` builds one from its pieces, `tracks.ts` the three), the car (`spec.ts`: the race tune, its grass version and its `A_LAT`), the racing line and par (`line.ts`), the round (`game.ts`), the ghost (`ghost.ts`), the frame (`draw.ts`), the component (`game.svelte`), the proof driver (`autopilot.ts`, tests only). Decided in the [wave 3 brainstorm](../brainstorm/2026-10-29_drive-hub-wave-3.md) ("F1 grip", "First cuts › Hot Lap"). Building: `Building.TRACK`, a chequered band on the roof.

### Tracks

Hand-laid, not seeded: each `TrackDef` is a list of straights and bends (`{ straight }`, `{ radius, degrees }`, positive turning right) traced from the origin heading east; the turns add up to one right-hand lap and the pieces close on themselves (a test holds the miss under 1 m; what's left is spread over the lap). Two straights per track were solved for the closure when it was laid. Each track has its own silhouette and pace, recognisable in the overview: a lap is 3.3–4.2 km with straights long enough to reach or near top speed and brake from it. `line` puts the start/finish line 150–180 m down the first straight so the grid behind it is straight. `runoff` (optional, at most the default 15 m) brings a street circuit's walls in close. `build_track` resamples the centreline every metre and keeps per sample: the heading, the normal (to the right), the signed curvature, a kerb flag (within 12 m of a bend tighter than 220 m), the edge (7.5 m half-width, plus the 1.5 m kerb where there is one) and the wall's distance on each side.

| Track | Length | Character | Par |
|-------|--------|-----------|-----|
| Meadow | 4204 m | fast and flowing: a long triangle of 160–400 m sweepers and two 1 km straights; the slowest corner is taken at about 220 km/h | 57.1 s |
| Harbour | 3626 m | a street circuit, 5 m of run-off: a 1.1 km straight into a 45 m hairpin, a staircase of 60 m right angles, a 45 m chicane; the hairpin at about 110 km/h | 64.8 s |
| Ridge | 3262 m | technical, a dog-bone: esses on both long sides, a double-apex right, a long left-hand sweeper, short straights | 51.8 s |

The first cut was three variations of a rounded rectangle, 450–657 m long, for the core's 50 km/h F1 car; the owner found them "basically the same" and the car "just funny", so the tracks were redrawn at full racing size with the car (2026-10-29).

**Walls.** Each side's wall sits up to 15 m of grass out from the asphalt's edge, but never past 0.85 of the point where another part of the track is as near (`|Δ|² / (2 Δ·n)` over the other samples): between two close straights the walls run either side of a grass strip, inside a bend the wall closes to a ring rather than a point. Only samples in the same or a neighbouring grid cell can pull a wall in (a cell is wider than any sample that could), so building a track is linear rather than quadratic in its length (20–50 ms). A minimum over ±6 samples and an average smooth it. Collision uses one 1 m thick rectangle per 2 m of wall, extruded along each piece's own perpendicular so it stays a rectangle in any bend; drawing uses one closed line per wall (`walls`), since a translucent stroke over many pieces shows their seams. The asphalt is drawn as one ring (both edges, even-odd fill) for the same reason. No bend is tighter than 45 m on the centreline, well inside the car's lock (tested under 0.6 of it).

`locate(track, point, hint)` is the nearest centreline point (index, arc length `s`, signed `lateral`), searched within 24 samples of the last index so close parts of the track can't swap; `progress` and `passes` measure forward along the loop.

### Grip

The core is kinematic: a car turns as tight at 50 km/h as standing still. The core's grip cap (Core › Grip) holds the wheel in every `step()`; Hot Lap's race spec sets `a_lat` = `A_LAT` = 25 m/s², a racing car's cornering (`spec.ts`). At 40 m/s that's about an eighth of lock; below about 14 m/s the full lock is allowed.

**The car** is a race tune of the core's `F1_CAR`, owned by the game (`SPEC` in `spec.ts`): 80 m/s cap (about 78 m/s, 282 km/h, reached against rolling resistance), 15 m/s² acceleration, 36 m/s² brakes, 0.6 s centre to lock. The garage's F1 car, which the yard and the parking games drive, keeps the core spec's parking pace (14 m/s, 6 and 12 m/s²). The first cut drove the core spec itself.

### The round

`create_game({ track, daily? })` puts the car on the grid, 9 m behind the line, in D. `advance(state, input, dt)`:

- **Grass.** With the body centre past the edge (half the car off), the next step uses `GRASS_SPEC`: the race car with a 25 m/s cap and 8 m/s² of drag, so flat out it loses about 30 km/h a second and settles near 40 km/h. The HUD says "On the grass". The lap still counts.
- **Walls.** Tested only when the car could reach one (its lateral plus 4 m past the wall's distance). A hit puts the car back where it was at speed 0 (the wheel and pedals keep moving), draws the contact rings for 1.2 s and voids the lap (`clean: false`). Back away and carry on; the thunk plays once per touch.
- **Laps.** A session is three laps. The first crossing of the line starts lap 1 (a short run-up from the grid); each lap must pass sector line 1 (a third of the way), sector line 2 and the line again, in order and going forward, so reversing over a line or cutting back counts nothing. Each crossing time is interpolated within the step, so a lap's time doesn't round to 1/120 s. After the third lap the session is done.
- **Ghost recording.** While a lap runs, `record_frame` appends the pose ten times a second, quantised to centimetres and milliradians (three integers a frame, about 15 kB of JSON a minute).

### Par: the racing line

`racing_line(track)`, cached per track:

1. **Minimum-curvature line** (`min_curvature`): every centreline sample slides along its normal, within the asphalt less half the car and 30 cm, to where the sum of squared second differences through its neighbours is least: projected Gauss–Seidel with over-relaxation (1.7), coarse to fine (every 16th point, then 8th, 4th, 2nd, every one; 400 sweeps each). Without the coarse passes it barely leaves the centreline.
2. **Speed profile** (`speed_profile`): each point's cap is `min(top, √(A_LAT / κ))`; a forward pass with the car's acceleration (`accel · (1 − v / max_forward) − rolling`, as `step` computes it), a backward pass with its brakes (`brake + rolling`), each twice round so the lap joins up.
3. **Par** is the flying lap's time on it, with the time at each sector line (`splits`).

The model is the game's own, so par is honest: the proof driver (`autopilot`, pure pursuit of the line with its speed, through `advance` and the grip cap) laps every track clean within 1–5 % of par (the pedals take a third of a second to press, which the profile doesn't model; Harbour, with the most hard stops, is furthest), and 6–8 % off at 95 % pace. It has a perfect hand on the wheel; a player on keys doesn't, so three stars allow 8 %.

### Scoring and saved data

- **Stars** per lap (`lap_stars`): a clean lap is one, within 20 % of par two, within 8 % three. A void lap earns none, and crossing the line on one plays no chime. The session's card shows its best clean lap and its stars, every lap's time (void ones marked), par and the saved best.
- **Ghost** (`f1/ghost-v2-{track}.json` in the app's data folder, ADR-035, through `storage.file`; `v2` since the tracks were redrawn, so a ghost from the first cut's tracks is never read): `{ ms, splits, frames }` of the best clean lap ever on that track, written when a lap beats it (`beats`). It is the track's best: the start card and the HUD read Best from it. A session races the ghost saved when it started (beating it mid-session doesn't swap the car ahead); it's drawn at 32 % opacity under the player at the same lap time, interpolated between frames. The HUD shows each sector's delta to the ghost's split as it's passed (green ahead, warning behind).
- **Daily**: the track of the day is `TRACK_IDS[daily_seed(day, "tight-spot/f1") % 3]`. Every clean lap on it is reported with `play.daily(day, ms, undefined, "f1")`; `daily_rank.f1` is `DailyRank.TIME`, so Game Hub keeps the quickest and lists "Drive Hub · F1".
- **Achievements**: `f1-first` (a clean lap), `f1-on-pace` (three stars), `f1-ghost` (beat a saved ghost), `f1-clean-session` (three clean laps), `f1-tracks` (a ghost on every track, goal 3), `f1-daily` (a clean lap on the daily).

### Camera

Before the start and on the done card, the whole circuit (`fit_span`, 3 m margin) clear of the card, its grass as the track's plot. Driving, it widens with speed from the F1 car's parking span (`view_span`, about 34 m across the shorter side) to 110 m at top speed (only with the shared setting **Zoom with speed**, on by default, `app.tight-spot.speed_zoom`; off, it keeps the parking span and the player's own zoom, and the look-ahead stays), no further, so the car stays readable: about 30 px long on a 600 px board, 20 px on a 390 px phone. The speed it zooms by is eased (`PACE_RATE`, 1.2 a second), so braking into a hairpin draws the view in over a second or so and a lift off the throttle doesn't pump it. The look-ahead runs only along the direction of travel: 2 s of the eased speed, at most 0.7 of the way from the middle of the clear part of the board to its edge in that direction (`reach`), so a straight along the board's longer side shows about 2.5 s ahead on a wide desktop board, while one along the shorter side (or any straight on an upright phone) shows about a second; the car stays on the board's centre line across. Following, the camera isn't kept inside the track's bounds (clamping held the car near the edge next to an outer straight, with the board's middle all infield), and the grass fills the whole board. The camera eases toward its target relative to the car (`follow`), so it doesn't trail the car at speed. Zoomed out by hand (pinch, −) below 3.4 px a metre, a ring of fixed screen size (12 px) fades in round the car, fully drawn at 2 px a metre. A heading-up camera would give a phone the length of its board ahead on every straight; it isn't built.

### Tests

`track.test.ts`: every track closes, its bends are within the lock and its walls clear of the kerbs, the grid sits behind the line, the racing line stays on the asphalt and inside the lock. `game.test.ts`: the grip cap, the grass, a wall voiding the lap, a whole session's laps and splits, the proof (every track lapped clean at three-star pace by `autopilot`), stars, bests, ghost frames. Under 2 s in all.

Played in the browser (Playwright, solo mode): keys from the grid over the line, onto the grass and into a wall (Void, rings), pause; a whole session and a second one against the saved ghost driven by the proof driver wired in for the check only (not shipped), on desktop and at 390 × 844 and 844 × 390 touch, Brand Dark and Brand Light. After the scale-up: flat out down the first straight and through the first corner on keys and on the touch pedals and wheel, and the proof driver round each track, at 1400 and 700 px wide, 390 × 844 and 844 × 390.

## Ferry Loader (ferry)

`games/ferry/`: the deck and queue generator (`deck.ts`), the round model and its rules (`game.ts`), the sailing planner and par (`plan.ts`, `par.worker.ts`, `par.ts`), the frame (`draw.ts`), the component (`game.svelte`), the proof (`prove.ts`, `planner.test.ts`). Decided in [the wave 3 brainstorm](../brainstorm/2026-10-29_drive-hub-wave-3.md) ("First cuts › Ferry Loader").

### The deck

`build_deck(seed, difficulty, count)`, pure. The ship runs west to east: a drive-through deck of three or four lanes (x from 0 at the stern to the bow wall, y from 0 on the port side), steel hull walls, a closed bow, a ramp 6 m long at the stern whose opening stops short of each side, water either side of the ramp, a quay behind it with fences, and marshalling lanes past the quay fence where the queue waits (drawn only, out of reach). Port is the top of the screen, starboard the bottom.

**The packing comes first.** The queue's 3–6 vehicles are drawn from the garage (city car, hatchback, SUV, pickup, van, box truck, city bus; at most one bus and two heavies). Every assignment of vehicles to lanes is enumerated (4⁶ = 4096 at most); the ones balanced within 60 % of the limit are kept, the shortest longest lane wins (one picked by the seed among those within 0.5 m of it), each lane's vehicles in a seeded order nose to tail with 0.7 m gaps (`PACK_GAP`) from the bow. A set with no balanced packing is dealt again. The deck is that longest lane plus an apron at the stern to swing in. The queue is the packing's slots frontmost first, so each vehicle has the open stern behind the ones already aboard. `Deck.plan` keeps the packing for the planner; the player may put any vehicle in any lane.

| | Roomy (0) | Tight (1) |
|---|---|---|
| Lanes | 3 (4 from five vehicles) | 4 |
| Lane width | 3.5 m (+0–0.1) | 3.1 m (+0–0.1) |
| Apron at the stern | 8 m | 4 m |
| Ramp opening short of each side | 0.2 m | 0.9 m |

Each vehicle comes onto the quay in P, nose 1.5 m short of the ramp's foot, lined up with the ramp's middle (`spawn_pose`): with an even number of lanes that's a lane line, so every vehicle has to be steered into its lane.

### Stowing

- `judge()` says whether the vehicle could stay where it stands: every body corner on the deck (off the ramp), within 5° of the bow, inside one lane, its body centre within 25 cm of the lane's middle (`CENTRED`), and its nose 0.3–1 m (`MIN_GAP`, `MAX_GAP`) short of the rear of the vehicle ahead in that lane, or of the bow wall when it's the first.
- **Shifting into P** after a drive (the first P on the quay counts for nothing, as in parking) stows it if `judge()` has no fault: it becomes game-owned solids (`SolidKind.CAR` with the vehicle's look, drawn by the core's parked-vehicle drawing), the scene is rebuilt once (one `Scene` object per stow, so the obstacle cache holds), and the next vehicle in the queue comes onto the quay with `create_car`. Otherwise P only parks, and the hint at the bottom says why: *Not on the deck yet*, *Not straight: 6.2° off*, *Across two lanes*, *Off centre: 32 cm to port*, *Too close: 12 cm to the van*, *Pull up: 1.8 m to the bow*. Enter does the same as P once it stands where it would stow.
- **Undo** (U, Backspace, or the Undo button: bottom right on a fine pointer, over the pedals on touch) takes the last stowed vehicle back to the quay. Each stow pushes a `Snapshot` (the spawn car, the queue index, the stowed list and the scene as they were when that vehicle came onto the quay) on `GameState.history`; undo pops one and spreads it over the state. The clock keeps running; the round counts the undos.
- **Contact ends the sailing**, like parking: the hull, the bow, the ramp's edges into the water, the quay fences and any stowed vehicle, through the core's `collide()`. The card names it ("Touched the ship's side", "Touched the city bus").
- Each lane shows its stop window, the 0.3–1 m behind what's ahead, dashed; the one the vehicle is in fills and pulses.
- **Parking sensors**, as in parking: read against the scene (the hull, the bow, the quay fences and every stowed vehicle) each frame, the front in D and the rear in R, with the beeps, the arcs at the bumper and the readout at the top right (under the speed on touch). Readings reset with each new round.

### Balance and scoring

- **Balance** (`moment`): the load's turning moment about the centre line as a share of the most it could be, Σ w·(y − W/2) / (Σ w · W/2), from −1 (all on the port edge) to 1. Weights in tonnes: city car 1.0, hatchback 1.3, SUV 2.2, pickup 2.3, van 2.8, box truck 7.5, bus 12. The gauge (P–S, top right; under the gear strip on a phone) reads it live with the vehicle in hand counted once it's on the deck; the green band is the limit, `BALANCE_LIMIT` = 12 %.
- **Stars** (`stars`): one for all aboard, one for the balance within the limit at the end, one for the time at or under the par time.
- **Par time** (`par_of`): `plan_sailing` drives the queue in order from the quay to its packing lanes; its path length at 1.8 m/s, plus 2.5 s per change of direction and 4 s per vehicle, times 1.25 for slack, rounded up to 5 s. It runs in a one-shot worker per round like the Warehouse's, keyed on `$derived(game.round)`; a sailing finished first is recorded at once and again with the time star when the par lands. Every such sailing waits on its own round's par (a list, and the earlier round's job is kept running), so starting and finishing another before the first par lands keeps both.
- Best: more stars, then time, per `level:N`, `sailing:N` (vehicles) and `daily:YYYY-MM-DD` (`record`).
- Levels (`level_round`): 12, three each of three to six vehicles, difficulty 0 → 1, seeds `hash(0xfe22, index)`. The saved level (`app.tight-spot.ferry.level`) is clamped to the open ones once the data file is read. Sailings (`sailing_round`): 3–6 vehicles and difficulty 0.15–0.85 from the seed. Daily (`daily_round`): five vehicles, difficulty 0.3–0.8, seed `daily_seed(day, "tight-spot/ferry")`, the same queue for everyone; it goes to Game Hub under `tight-spot/ferry` scored as stars lost, then time.

### Every sailing can be loaded

`plan_leg` searches each vehicle from its spawn with hybrid A* only until it is lined up behind its packing slot (within 1°, its centre within 8 cm of the lane's middle, short of the slot) and a straight run up stops it 0.6–0.65 m short of what's ahead (`straight_in`), with the bodies grown 5 cm, the vehicles before it where their legs left them. `prove()` drives every leg again in 15 cm pieces with the real bodies and no margin, judges each stop with `judge()`, and checks the balance at the end. `planner.test.ts`: levels 1, 4, 8 and 12, two random sailings and the daily of 2026-10-29, 0.1–3 s each. All 12 levels and 60 sampled sailings proved while the generator was tuned.

`game.test.ts`: the same seed builds the same ferry; every level's packing is balanced, on the deck and touches nothing; each spawn is clear and on the ramp's middle; tighter levels have narrower lanes; the gap window's edges, off centre, crooked, across a line and off the deck; P stows and the next comes on, the gap measured to the one ahead; P before a drive only parks; undo restores the snapshot and keeps the clock; contact; balance, stars, bests and levels.

Played through in a browser from the arrow keys and P under Playwright's fake clock: level 1 loaded down the middle lane with one undo, two stars, the achievement.

## Mow Day (mower)

`games/mower/`: the garden builder (`garden.ts`), the cutting deck (`deck.ts`), the coverage grid (`coverage.ts`), the coverable lawn (`reach.ts`), the round model (`game.ts`), the measuring worker (`measure.ts`, `measure.worker.ts`), the frame and the lawn layer (`draw.ts`), the component (`game.svelte`). Decided in [the wave 3 brainstorm](../brainstorm/2026-10-29_drive-hub-wave-3.md) ("Mower deck", "First cuts › Mow Day"). The vehicle is the garage's zero-turn mower, unchanged.

### The garden

`build_garden(seed, difficulty)`, pure. A rectangle of lawn inside a 0.7 m hedge, 11–21 m by 9–15 m by difficulty and seed; a 3.6 × 4.4 m paved patio in one south corner with the 2.4 × 2 m shed against the hedges, the mower starting on the patio in front of the shed's doors facing the lawn. The lawn is the garden less the patio, as two convex rectangles. On it: one or two flower beds along the hedges, up to three island beds (round or oblong), one to three trees and maybe a pond (an ellipse, more likely at higher difficulty). Everything but the border beds keeps `gap` (3 m easy, 2 m hard, always over the mower's width) from the others and from the hedges, and nothing goes near the patio or the run-out in front of it. The hedges, the shed (`SolidKind.WALL`), each trunk (a 12-gon `POST`, 0.25–0.4 m) and the pond (`WATER`) are solids; the beds are not.

**Contact.** The core's `collide()`; a solid is a bump, as in the Warehouse (`bump`): the step is undone, the mower stops in gear, the contact rings flash for `BUMP_FLASH_MS` (1.2 s, `bump_flash`), the hint names it (`bump_hint`: "Bumped a tree", "the pond", "the shed", "the hedge", then ": back off"), and `BUMP_COST_MS` (5 s) goes on the clock. Pressing on is the same bump (`bumped`) until the mower is `BUMP_CLEAR` (0.3 m) from where it bumped. The count shows in the HUD chip, the status bar and the done card; there is no crash card. A bed under any part of the mower's body is flattened instead (below).

### The deck

Game-owned, so `VehicleSpec` stays as it is: `DECKS` maps a vehicle to its cutting rectangle in its own frame, the zero-turn's three blades 0.54–1.02 m ahead of its drive axle and 1.44 m across. Each physics step cuts `swept(from, to)`, the convex hull of the deck's two footprints (exact for a straight step, close enough for the 8 ms steps of a pivot).

### Coverage

A grid of 15 cm cells over the garden (`create_grid`), typed arrays: the ground (none, lawn, bed, by cell centre; cells under a solid are none), coverable, cut (long, light, dark), the odometer at the first cut, overlapped, trampled, plus `marks` for the drawing only (below): the cells stay the score.

- **Stripes.** A cut cell takes the shade of the way the deck was moving along whichever axis it mostly moved: east or south light, west or north dark, so up-and-down passes alternate whichever way the lawn is mown.
- **Overlap.** A cell cut again once the deck has gone more than 1.2 m (`REPASS`) since its first cut counts as overlapped, once; less is the same pass going over it. The overlap is overlapped coverable cells over coverable cells.
- **Flowers.** A bed cell under the body is trampled once and costs three cells of mown lawn (`BED_COST`).
- **Coverage** = (mown coverable cells − 3 × trampled) / coverable cells.
- **Too many flowers.** Once the best the round can still reach (every coverable cell mown, less 3 × trampled) drops under 90 % (`out_of_reach`), the round ends: status `FLATTENED`, card "Too many flowers flattened" with what they took off, Try again (and New lawn on random lawns). Checked on each new trampled cell and when the measure lands (`apply_mask`), so a mask that arrives late still ends a round that was already lost.

### The coverable lawn

`flood` runs the planner's lattice from the start, breadth first: straight steps of 0.4 m both ways and pivots of one 15° heading bin (checked halfway too), the body grown by the planner's 5 cm, solids and beds both blocking; states are binned by 0.4 m and 24 headings. The lawn under the deck at every pose it reached is coverable. A step is shorter than the deck and a pivot's swing shorter than its width, so the footprints join. A skid steer's pivot is why the coarse lattice is enough (it throws for any other drive). It leaves out what the mower can't reach without the flowers: the last 10–15 cm along a hedge, corners the long nose can't get into, lawn shut in by beds; 85–93 % of the lawn is coverable. The flood takes 60–300 ms, so the game runs it in a one-shot worker per round (`work_out_lawn`, keyed on `$derived(game.round)` like the Warehouse's par); the round may start before it lands, and `apply_mask` counts what was already mown. Again reuses the mask. If the worker fails, the error goes to a toast and `measure_fallback` counts every lawn cell as coverable (par from that), so the round can still be finished; corners the deck can't reach then count against the player, which 90 % leaves room for.

### Scoring

- **Finish** (F, Space, or the Finish button over the pedals on touch, bottom right on a fine pointer) is lit from 90 % coverage. Every coverable cell mown with no flowers flattened ends the round by itself.
- Stars: one for finishing, one for an overlap of 25 % or less (`OVERLAP_LIMIT`), one for the time (bumps included) at or under par.
- **Par** (`par_time`): the coverable area in passes 1.2 m apart (a half-lock turn's shift) at 2.6 m/s, 4 s a turn at the end of each pass along the garden's longer side, 8 s round each tree, island bed and pond, 6 s off the patio, then ×1.25 and up to the next 5 s. An ideal up-and-down run over a bare lawn of the same size takes about 65–70 % of it with 12–13 % overlap.
- Past 80 % an arrow on the edge of the view points to the nearest long grass left (every 0.4 s). Running over flowers shows "Off the flowers" for 1.5 s.
- Best per `level:N`, `daily:YYYY-MM-DD` and one for random lawns: more stars, then time; the best coverage and least overlap are kept separately.
- Levels (`level_round`): 12, difficulty 0 → 1, seeds `hash(0x3077, index)`. Lawns (`lawn_round`): difficulty 0.1–0.9 from the seed. Daily: difficulty 0.3–0.8, seed `daily_seed(day, "tight-spot/mower")`, reported through `play.daily(day, ms, 3 − stars, "mower")`.

### Drawing

`LawnLayer` draws the lawn as vectors, not cells, on an offscreen canvas at about the screen's resolution (CSS pixels per metre times the device pixel ratio, rounded up to a step of √2 and capped at 4096 px a side and 8 M pixels), drawn under the lawn's outline. It fills the long grass, then replays `grid.marks` in order: runs of deck shapes per stripe tone (`mark_cut`, called from `advance` beside `mow`), and the mower's footprints that flattened a bed cell. A run's last shape is the hull of the deck at the chunk's first pose and the current one, regrown each step until the deck has gone `CHUNK` (0.3 m) or turned `CHUNK_TURN` (0.08 rad), then a new one starts, so a long round stays a few thousand shapes (a ten-minute round: under a thousand) and a full repaint takes a millisecond or two. A step paints only the shapes since the last frame (the open one again); a new round or a theme repaints the lot, and so does a zoom past a resolution step, once the camera has eased to within 2 % of its new scale (`settled`; before that the old layer is drawn scaled, unless the round has under 400 shapes). The cut edges are therefore straight and the stripes clean at any zoom, where the old one-pixel-a-cell image showed the 15 cm grid as a smoothed staircase. Colours are theme tokens resolved per theme (`read_lawn`): one green, `--success` 52 % into the background, moved in OKLCH lightness with relative colour syntax, so cut grass is lighter than the long grass on both themes: long grass −0.03, the light stripe (east or south) +0.08 at 1.15× chroma, the dark stripe (west or north) +0.045. The long grass has a grain from 36 px per metre: one short blade (5 to 9 cm, 2.2 cm wide) every 16 cm or so in a tone 0.013 darker, placed and leaned by a hash, so it reads as a calm texture, not cell noise. Beds are soil with flowers in the theme's accent tokens; flattened beds are drawn over them, clipped to the beds. Crowns are drawn over the mower at 70 % so it shows under the branches; trunks under it. The shed is a pitched board roof.

`garden.test.ts` (determinism, the start is clear and on the patio, nothing overlaps), `coverage.test.ts` (cells, stripes, overlap once and only after `REPASS`, trampling, the drawing's chunked marks, the sweep), `game.test.ts` (every level at least 85 % coverable, the daily's garden, driving off the patio mows a dark stripe, a solid is one bump until backed off, finishing and stars, bests and levels, when the flowers put 90 % out of reach, the fallback mask). Played end to end in Chromium from keys (level 1: a closed-loop driver over `DriveInput` and `advance` in Node turned into key presses, replayed under Playwright's clock) to 90 % and the done card.
## Valet Rush (valet)

`games/valet/`: the street (`street.ts`), the arrival schedule and the shift model with its rules (`game.ts`), the frame (`draw.ts`), the component (`game.svelte`), tests (`game.test.ts`). Decided in [the wave 3 brainstorm](../brainstorm/2026-10-29_drive-hub-wave-3.md) ("Contact", "Shared open lot", "First cuts › Valet Rush"). Building `Building.VALET_STAND`.

### The street

`build_street(seed)`: the open lot (`open_world`, `core/lot.ts`) sized for the van, the biggest customer car (longest, widest, widest turn), so every customer car fits every bay; the lots' bays, underground garages included, and the kerb bays along the east–west roads are the ones parking's open-lot sweep proves for the van, and a car left in a kerb bay is parked like any other (`parked_in` takes any convex bay). The kerb bays never touch the north and south roads, so the queue, the stand, the return box and the coach bays keep their lanes. On the south road, just past the crossroads: the stand's booth on the east kerb, the queue in the east lane (cars nose north, 1.2 m apart, front car 2.5 m into the road) and the return box in the west lane (`returns`, either way round, long enough for the bus). The bus can't turn at a crossroads of 8 m roads, so it drives straight on into a **coach bay** up the north road, nose to the end, and later backs straight out to the stand. That road may end at a lot's gate 20 m up and may have gates down its sides; a coach bay goes in each lane with no gate beside it, or in the east lane when both have one. Coach bays take only the bus, and the bus only coach bays, so a car can't lock the bus out.

### The shift

- **Arrivals** (`schedule(seed)`): pure, 300 of them. The first is waiting at the start; the gap between two shrinks from 60 s to 24 s over the first 12 (each ×0.7–1.3 from the seed). Cars are hatchback, city car, SUV, pickup and van (weights 3, 2, 2, 1.5, 1.5); from the fifth arrival a bus at 14 % a time, at least 8 arrivals after the last. Each owner stays 70–150 s (the bus 50–80 s) from when the car is taken.
- **Statuses**: ready, stand (between cars; the clock runs and cars keep arriving), driving, paused (`resume` says which to go back to), over. The fourth car waiting ends the shift (`QUEUE_LIMIT`), at whatever moment it pulls up, a walk included.
- **Take** (T, or the stand card's button): the queue's first car becomes the vehicle (`create_car` with its spec), its ticket's return time starts. The queue behind keeps its place while the car in hand is still in the east lane (`held`), then moves up.
- **Arrivals never land on the valet.** A queued car on the street is a `Standing` (`lined`): once it has pulled up it stays put and solid, however close the car in hand comes. A standing car never leaves the street and never backs up (when the car in hand drives back into the lane the queue's places move back, and the cars already further up stay there). It moves up to a free place only when the strip from that place, 0.8 m ahead of it (`ARRIVAL_CLEAR`), back to where it stands is clear of the car in hand and every car ahead has moved up; otherwise it stays where it is, solid, and every step checks again. A car that hasn't pulled up yet (a new arrival) only does once its place grown by 0.8 m and the lane behind it to the street's end, 0.8 m wider each side, are clear of the car in hand and no car ahead is still waiting to move up; until then it waits off the street (`out`, not drawn, not solid, still counted in the queue). The board slides a car in over 0.9 s from the street's end, or from its old place when it moves up (`since`, `from`); it's solid at its place from the start, which the check has just found clear. So a car never pops in on or beside the car in hand, and never dents it.
- **Park**: P while standing in a free bay, lined up within 5° (`parked_in`, the same test as parking's), ends the job. The car becomes the game's solids (`car_solids`, like the ferry's stowed vehicles) and its precision is kept (`fit()`). The valet walks back: the clock jumps by the straight-line distance from the car to the stand at 2.5 m/s (`walk_ms`), and arrivals during it count.
- **Fetch** (F): the owner who has waited longest. The clock jumps by the walk out to the car, then the parked car comes off the scene and becomes the vehicle; P lined up in the return box hands it back.
- **Leave it here** (L, Enter, or the button at the bottom of the board): a fetched car, once driven, stopped in P lined up in any bay it may use (a lot bay or a kerb bay; a coach bay for the bus) can be left there (`can_leave`, `leave`). It's parked again where it stands, with the precision of this park, its ticket and its owner's wait unchanged, so the owner is still waiting and it can be fetched again; the valet walks back to the stand as after a park (not one more parked). That's how to switch cars when the queue blocks the way to the return box: leave the fetched car, take the queue's first, or fetch another owner's. A new car needs no such button: P in a bay parks it.
- **Contact** is a dent, never the end: the step that touched is undone (the car stays where it was, speed 0, the new pedal and gear kept), the score drops by 5 (`DENT_COST`), the car in hand loses its tip, the dent counter goes up, the clean run (`clean`, cars handed back since the last dent, for Not a scratch) starts again and contact rings flash for 1.2 s. Pressing on against the same thing is one dent: a new one counts only once the car has moved 0.3 m from where it touched (`CLEAR`).
- **Tip** (`tip_for`), paid on hand-back: 10, plus up to 10 for a quick return (all of it if the owner hasn't waited, none after 60 s at the stand), plus up to 5 for the park's precision; the bus doubles it; a dented car pays nothing.
- **Score** = tips − 5 × dents. The final score is never below zero.
- Hints on P: not in a bay, not straight (degrees off), the bus's coach bay, at the stand with a car to park, not yet in the return box. A fetched car lined up in a bay shows the Leave button instead, and the job line says "Leave the … here, or bring it to the box".

### Saved data and the daily

`valet.json` in the app data folder: `{ best }`, the best `{ score, ms, handed, dents }` for random shifts (`shifts`) and per `daily:YYYY-MM-DD`; a higher score wins, then a shorter shift, the same as Game Hub's `SCORE_DESC`, so both show the same run. The daily's seed is `daily_seed(day, "tight-spot/valet")` (the lot and the schedule from it), reported through `play.daily(day, ms, score, "valet")` and ranked `DailyRank.SCORE_DESC`, higher first.

### Screen

The camera frames the whole street between cars (so the owners' cars show) and follows the car in hand. The HUD: the gear strip, speed and the clock; the job line; the score and dents; the queue as four slots (warning colour from three); a chip per parked car with its owner's countdown or how long they've waited (four at most). On a phone the queue, score and timers go under the gear strip. Each parked customer car carries its countdown on the board, the coach bays a Bus label, the stand a Valet label; the return box glows on a fetch and a pointer at the board's edge leads to it (and to the coach bay with the bus). The stand card offers Fetch and Take, primary on whichever is wiser (Take once three wait); Enter fetches if an owner is back, else takes. With a fetched car stopped in a bay, a "Leave it here" button (44 px tall on touch, with the L key hint on a fine pointer) sits at the bottom of the board where the hints go.

### Tests

`game.test.ts`: every customer car pulls up clear and fits a bay and the return box (seeds 1–3); the schedule is pure and speeds up, buses at least 8 apart; take, park, fetch and hand back pay the tip; a contact is one dent while pushing on, and starts the clean run again; a waiting car left out under the car in hand comes back once it's clear; arrivals while the valet crosses the queue's lane on the way to the box wait off the street (no overlap, no dent) and pull up once the car is in the box; a car that has pulled up stays put with the valet driving right beside it; cars standing in the queue stay on the street (none out, no overlap, no dent) when the valet pulls sideways out of the lane; a fetched car left in another bay is parked again with its owner still waiting, then the queue's first can be taken (clear of the scene) or the same car fetched from its new bay; an equal score ranks the shorter shift first; tip arithmetic; the fourth arrival ends the shift. A par valet (3 m/s along the roads plus 10 s into a bay, the nearest free bay, the queue first once two wait, walks as the game times them) keeps the queue under four for five minutes on seeds 1–3, in about 0.2 s; left to run it lasts 8–10 minutes.

## Rodeo

`games/rodeo/`: the course builder (`course.ts`), the run model and its rules (`game.ts`), the frame (`draw.ts`), the component (`game.svelte`), the proof (`prove.ts`, `planner-solo.test.ts`, `planner-rigs.test.ts`). Decided in the [wave 3 brainstorm](../brainstorm/2026-10-29_drive-hub-wave-3.md) ("First cuts › Rodeo", contact table). Building `Building.ARENA`.

### The course

`build_course(seed, spec)`, pure: one seed gives a course of the same shape for every vehicle, scaled to it. Inputs are the rig's length and widest body (`rig_length`, `rig_width`), the length its trailers add, and its turning circle (`turning_radius`, at least 0.9 of the width and 1.2 m, so a skid steer still gets room to line up). A walled arena with two lanes, west to east:

- **Gates.** The vehicle starts at the west end of the north lane facing east, behind a painted start line. Three gates down the lane, each offset across it by up to 0.35 × width + 0.25 × turning radius; a gate is two cones `width + 2 × clear + cone` apart (clear 0.3 × width, at least 0.4 m, ×0.95–1.3 from the seed). The gap to the next gate is the S-bend that jump takes (2√(radius × jump) × 1.15), plus half the rig, 0.7 of any trailer length, 2 m, and for a frame steer twice the length its joint needs to bend lock to lock (`2 × max_steer / FRAME_RAMP`), ×1.05–1.35.
- **U-turn.** At the east end. The lanes are 2 × turning radius ×1.05–1.3 apart (plus 0.6 of any trailer length), at least as far as the gates' swing and the slalom need; the east fence is past where the body's front corner swings.
- **Slalom.** Four or five cones on the south lane's centre line, westbound, passed on alternate sides (the first side from the seed). Spacing: 2√(2 × cone room × radius) + 0.6 × length + 0.8 × trailer length (+ the frame ramp), ×1.05–1.3, where cone room is half the width plus clear plus half a cone.
- **Stop box and reverse gate.** Past the slalom a straight run of at least the rig's length plus 2 m, then the reverse gate (narrower: `width + 1.6 × clear + cone`), then the stop box: rig length + max(0.7, 0.12 × length) slack, width + max(0.7, 0.35 × width), ×0.85–1.3 from the seed. The reverse gate sits on the box's line, shifted across by up to 0.35 × width (0.15 for a trailer, none for a chain of two links or more).

`Course.steps` lists them in order: three `gate`s, the `slalom` cones, `stop`, `reverse`. A gate is a segment `a`–`b` with the `normal` it is crossed along. A slalom cone's segment runs from the cone to the fence on the side it's passed, so crossing the cone's line anywhere on that side counts.

### The run

- `advance` steps the vehicle, then: a jackknife or the fence (`collide()` against the course's solids) ends the run; any body overlapping a standing cone knocks it over (`touched`), it lies rolled off the way the rig was going and no longer collides, and each one adds `PENALTY_MS` (5 s) to the time (`total_ms`); then the step in hand is judged.
- Steps count in order only. A gate or a cone counts when the vehicle's nose (`nose()`, a frame steer's front frame) crosses its segment along its normal between two steps (`crossed`); the reverse gate only while backing (speed below 0), so the whole rig has passed when the nose does. The stop box counts standing (under `STANDING`) with every body, trailers and forks included, inside it (`in_box`). The last step finishes the run.
- **Parking sensors**, as in parking: read against the fence and every cone still standing (`sensed(state)`, one `Obstacles` per cones array, so it's rebuilt only when a cone goes down), the front in D and the rear in R, with the beeps, the arcs and the readout at the top right (under the speed on touch). Readings reset with each new run.
- HUD: the time with penalties, the cones down, the step in hand ("Gate 2/3", "Slalom 3/5 · pass it on the right", "Stop in the box", "Back out through the gate"). The next gate's line and cones glow, a chevron shows which way through and which side of each cone, the off-screen pointer leads to the next one. The crab chip shows for the telehandler.
- **Bests** per vehicle and course (`vehicle:course`, quicker wins, penalties included) and per daily day, in a file (below). Twelve courses (`course_round`, seeds `hash(0x40de0, n)`); Next course goes round them.
- **Daily** (`daily_round`): seed `daily_seed(day, "tight-spot/rodeo")`, the vehicle from it too (`hash(seed, 3)`) among the garage's vehicles with at most one link (`DAILY_VEHICLES`, everything but the experimental shelf). Ranked by time (`DailyRank.TIME`), the cones knocked as the score; Game Hub lists it as Drive Hub · Rodeo.
- **Experimental.** A rig of two or more links (`experimental()`: the drawbar truck, the road train, the baggage train and the tractor with two trailers) has no proven course, so the picker leaves it off its garage shelf and lists it on a shelf of its own, Experimental (`SHELVES`, `rodeo_shelf`); its start card says the course isn't proven and may be impossible. Its runs keep bests but count toward no achievement, and the daily never deals one.
- Achievements: a finished run, a clean one, one towing a trailer, a run with a vehicle from every garage shelf (progress to 7, the daily's vehicle included: its best records it), the daily. None for an experimental rig.
- Below `@md` the toolbar's Courses button is hidden, so the daily's start card carries it.

### Every course can be driven clean

`prove(state)` plans the course step by step from the real start with every cone a solid and the bodies grown 5 cm, then drives each leg again in 15 cm pieces with the real bodies and no margin and judges it the way a run is: every step done in order, no cone touched, no fence, no jackknife. Per leg, for this leg only, thin rails run along the gate's line outside it across its lane, so the search can't count a way round. A gate is one leg that ends just through it, square (every heading within 0.6 rad) and with joint and hitches straight, so the next leg isn't a dead end. A slalom cone is two legs, up to it lined up on its side and then past it (forward only), since one search could reach its line by turning round short of it; the first cone gets the U-turn before them as two more legs, to its middle and round it, since a search aimed at the cone floods the lane first. Towing rigs search forward only. The proofs (`planner-solo.test.ts`: the hatchback on courses 1 and 2, the bus, the ladder truck, the telehandler and the skid-steer loader on course 1; `planner-rigs.test.ts`: the car and trailer on course 2) take about 5 s together.

`game.test.ts`: the same seed builds the same course and a bigger vehicle a bigger one, the step order, every vehicle starts clear with a box longer than its rig, the daily's vehicles; gate crossing (inside, outside, the wrong way), the reverse gate only backing, the stop box only standing inside; a knocked cone adds 5 s once and the run goes on, the fence ends it; the sensors hear a standing cone and not a knocked one; bests, the shelves done with a daily and without the chains, and the experimental shelf holding every chain and only chains.

### The rigs

`TOW` (game-owned, `game.ts`): the same tug and airliner, 2.5 m/s forward, the link at rest 0 with its hinge 6.6 m behind the tug's rear axle, so the tug's back is about a metre clear of the nose. Pulling makes the fold settle on its own; the aircraft's wheels cut inside the tug's.

The tug is drawn by the core's `player` with the baggage train's look and no links, the towbar from its front (pushing) or back (towing) to the nose gear, the aircraft by the game: shadow, wings and tailplanes in `pavement`, the fuselage in the lightest car paint, engines in `steel`, the fin and wingtips in the player's colour (a parked one's in a car colour), the flight deck's windows, the gear faintly through the body, and a red beacon that flashes once it moves. The nose stops 5.5–6.1 m off the terminal. Every stand has a jet bridge (a rotunda and a tunnel to a parked aircraft's front door, or pulled back on an empty stand and on ours), lead-in lines that curve onto the centreline both ways, and a stop bar; the neighbour on the other side is there 80 % of the time, stands further along 55 %. Solids come in two heights, game-owned like the Warehouse's tiers (the core's scene and `collide()` are untouched): `solids` is what stands on the ground (the terminal, the blast fence and the apron's ends, the bridges, parked aircraft's fuselages, engines and gear, the ground kit), `high` (a scene of its own) what reaches wing height (the terminal, the fence and ends, the bridges, parked aircraft's wings and tailplanes). Tall things are in both. `parts` maps each solid to what it is for the crash card. Stands are numbered west to east from a seeded 21–61, remote stands R1, R2, … ATC's clearance (`clearance()`) is per phase: "Hopper 417, stand 23, push back approved, face west." / "Bags for Hopper 417, stand 23, aft hold. Train cleared to the aircraft." / "Hopper 417, tow approved, remote stand R2 to stand 23." Pushback's apron is exactly what it was before the other jobs (the frame refactor changed no output).

### Baggage run

`build_baggage` (`ramp.ts`): Pushback's apron with the baggage hall in place of a stand two or one slots either side of ours (no stand, bridge or aircraft there; a door in the terminal with a BAGS sign), our aircraft parked on its stand (bridge on), and `ours`: its low parts, a power unit at the nose, cones at its wingtips and a belt loader out from the aft hold door (12.8 m behind the main gear on the side away from the bridge). They're solids only while the train drives (`scene_of`). The train starts at the hall door facing out, its carts in a line behind it. The mark is the middle cart's centre, 14.3 m behind the main gear, on a lane 7.7–8.9 m off the fuselage's centreline: clear of the tailplane, the wing's trailing edge 1.2 m ahead of the tug at the mark.

The route (`route()`): straight out of the hall, a quarter circle onto a road behind the tails (5.5–9 m behind them), along it with a slalom of up to 3.2 m (two waves of about 30 m when the road is long enough), a quarter circle up the lane, and on to the mark: always forward, since three carts can't really back up. `run_route` (`train.ts`) drives it: pure pursuit 3 m ahead with the tug, the wheel's change per 15 cm piece limited, until the middle cart reaches the mark once the tug is on the last straight. The generator drives the route first against the ground solids grown 25 cm (the train fits under wings and tailplanes), trying a wider road and rounder turns until one is clean (another seed if none is), keeps the train's bodies every few pieces as its sweep, and only then places kit: the parked aircraft's service kit (power unit, belt loader, cones at the wingtips and tail), a cone or power unit on the road at each slalom crest, and a few more beside the route, each kept only if it stands clear of the sweep by the difficulty's clearance (1.6 m easy, 0.5 m tight) and touches nothing, wings included. So every baggage run can be driven forward, and the par comes from the same driver.

Done (`train_done`): in P with the middle cart's centre within 1 m of the mark and the cart within 10° of parallel to the aircraft. P elsewhere says what's off: carts not lined up, too far out, short of or past the hold door. A cart folded past its 75° hinge ends the round as a jackknife ("Jackknifed at the second hinge"). The camera follows the train at a 34 m span.

### Tow to gate

`build_tow` (`ramp.ts`): our stand is the gate, empty with its bridge pulled back; the fence moves out to make room for a row of remote stands past the taxiway, 50–86 m along from the gate on a seeded side, the aircraft on the middle one facing the taxiway with the tug ahead, the others taken 40–85 % of the time. Done is Pushback's judge the other way round: the nose gear within 1 m of the gate's stop mark along the lead-in line, both gears within 0.5 m of that line, within 5° of facing the terminal.

The driver (`follow_tow`, `plan.ts`): a track for the main gear (straight off the remote stand, a quarter circle of radius R onto the centreline, along it, a quarter circle up the lead-in), the nose gear's track a wheelbase ahead along its tangent, and the tug's way from the towbar's equation solved backwards from the gate (forwards it's unstable); the tug pure-pursues that way 2.5 m ahead. `plan_tow` keeps the shortest of radii 16–28 m that ends done. Steering the tug to the trailer's wanted curvature directly folds the towbar away: with the hinge behind the tug's axle, the aircraft first turns the wrong way.

### Turnaround

A baggage run to our aircraft on Pushback's apron (bridge pulled back, kit kept out of the area the push can sweep), then `swap_to_push`: the train, the kit round our aircraft and the aircraft's own solids leave, `create_car` puts the pushback tug at the nose, the status goes back to ready with the clock and the changes of direction carried over, and the card says "Bags on board" with the push clearance (read aloud on Push back). Stars and par span the whole job.

### The round

- **Start**: Start (Enter, a pedal or the wheel) reads the clearance aloud through the speech service, owned by the window, when the game's sound is on; turning sound off, a new round or closing the game stops it.
- **Contact**: any touch ends the round, the card naming both ("A wingtip touched a parked aircraft", "The baggage train touched a belt loader", "The main gear touched …"). Heights decide what can touch: the tug and the train meet only the ground set, so they drive under wings and tailplanes; with an aircraft (`contact(near_of(state), …)`, a broad phase per height), the tug's body and the aircraft's low parts meet the ground set and its wings and tailplanes the high set (`collide_shape`); the fuselage, taller than a neighbour's wing root, meets both; the train through the core's `collide` against the ground set and our aircraft's low parts. The baggage run's start card says the train fits under wings and tailplanes. Past the 75° nose gear lock ends it too ("Nose gear past its steering lock"). The chain doesn't collide with itself.
- **Done**: in P (or Enter, standing) on the job's mark (above; Pushback's is the nose gear within 1 m of the stop mark along the centreline, both gears within 0.5 m of it, within 5° of the instructed heading). Otherwise P says what's off (`stop_hint`).
- **Stars**: one for done, one for no change of direction (`moves` counts D↔R over the whole job), one for the time at or under par.
- **Par** (`job_par`, in a worker per round): the job's driver at a careful pace, times 1.25, rounded up to 5 s: pushes and tows at 1.3 m/s, the baggage run at 2.2 m/s, a turnaround both added up.
- HUD: the gear strip, the job's line ("Face west · stop mark 41 m", "Aft hold · 35 m", "Stand 23 · stop mark 109 m"), and with an aircraft the nose gear's angle, amber within 15° of the lock. With an aircraft the camera frames the wingspan (54 m across the shorter side at zoom 1); before the start it frames the rig and its goal, whose outline is drawn dashed (the aircraft at its stop bar, or the middle cart beside the glowing hold door).
- Random rounds (difficulty 0.15–0.85), levels per job (difficulty 0 → 1; Pushback's seeds `hash(0x9b5c, index)` as before, the others' `hash(0x9b5c, salt, index)`), and one daily: a turnaround (difficulty 0.3–0.8, seed `daily_seed(day, "tight-spot/airport")`), reported to Game Hub as stars lost, then time.
- Achievements: Off blocks (a push, alone or in a turnaround), One smooth push, Bags on board, Towed in, Turned around, Cleared on time (three stars on any job), Ramp lead (every pushback level) and Daily departure (the daily turnaround), ids `airport-…`.

### Every job can be done

The core's hybrid A* didn't find a push in 120 000 expansions (two minutes): the state space of a 40 m pushed chain is too big for its grid. `plan.ts` is a pushback driver instead, the way the job is done: the main gear follows a track (straight back off the stand, a quarter circle of radius R onto the taxiway, then the centreline); an outer loop asks for the curvature that brings it back onto the track (lateral and heading gains, about a 50 m period, critically damped), `fold_for` turns that into the steady fold that makes it from the towing kinematics, and an inner loop picks the tug's wheel angle that closes the fold on it, its change per 15 cm piece limited to about what the wheel does at a pushback's pace. It pushes in 15 cm pieces with every body grown 30 cm and the lock less 8°, until the nose gear reaches the mark, and `plan_push` keeps the shortest of nine radii (16 to 40 m) that ends done. No change of direction is ever needed. The towing driver uses the same margins; the baggage driver steps with the real bodies (its generator already kept 25 cm).

`planner.test.ts` replays the push and tow drivers' moves with the real bodies and no margin and judges the end as a round would, and runs the baggage driver from the train's start: pushback levels 1, 6 and 12 and two random pushes, baggage levels 1 and 8 and a random run, tow levels 1 and 8 and a random tow, turnaround level 8 and the daily of 2026-10-29 (both parts), about 4 s in all. `game.test.ts`: the apron is deterministic and both ways turn up, heights (a tug under a parked wing and tailplane is clear, into a fuselage, an engine or a main gear leg is a hit, our wingtip into a neighbour's wing is a hit), each rig starts clear, the goals touch nothing, the judging at its edges for the aircraft and the train, the turnaround's swap (status, clock and moves carried, the push tug clear), the lock, crash titles, stars, bests and per-job levels. While building, the baggage generator found a clean route for 120 of 120 seeds across difficulties and the tow driver a tow for 14 of 14.

## Driving Test (test)

`games/test/`: the town (`town.ts`), the routes and the examiner's prompts (`route.ts`), the fault checks (`rules.ts`), the round (`game.ts`), the frame (`draw.ts`), the component (`game.svelte`), the proof driver (`driver.ts`, tests only). Decided in [ADR-089](../adrs/089-driving-test.md) and [its brainstorm](../brainstorm/2026-10-29_driving-test.md). Building `Building.TEST_CENTRE`, a dashed centre line and a zebra on the roof. The car is the hatchback with its top speed raised to 20 m/s (`SPEC`), so the town's limits can be broken; it has no grip cap (a normal car keeps full lock at any speed) and signals through the core signals (Core › Controls › Signals).

### The town

Hand-made data, built once (`town()`), right-hand traffic, about 470 × 300 m. Thirteen nodes on a 140 × 100 m grid: six junctions (T junctions at B, D, I and J, a crossroads with lights at E, a roundabout at F), two driveways into the test centre's car park on the north road (Q1, Q2) and five bends. Seventeen roads join them, each 7 m wide: one lane each way, or two lanes one way on the one-way street B→E. Limits 50 km/h, 30 km/h on the two southern roads (the school), 10 km/h in the car park.

- **Lane graph.** Every road gives a lane per direction and place (`{road}>{node}:{index}`, 0 the rightmost), from mouth to mouth, sampled every 0.5 m with headings. A junction's lanes join each incoming lane to every outgoing lane on another arm (no U-turns) with a cubic whose handles reach 0.55 of the way to where the two headings cross (a quarter circle for a square turn); on a two-lane approach the right lane doesn't turn left and the left lane doesn't turn right. Each lane carries its `limit`, its `next` lanes, its `turn` (left, right, straight) and the `control` at its end (stop, give way, lights with a phase group).
- **Junction geometry.** Mouths sit 8.5 m from the node (the road's half width plus a 5 m kerb radius), so a turn's kerb is a quarter circle about the turn's own centre and a car on the lane keeps 0.85 m off it. A corner between two arms is rounded; a missing arm's side is straight kerb.
- **The roundabout.** Island 8 m, ring lane on an 11.5 m circle, asphalt to 15 m, mouths 22 m out with flared entries. Each arm has an entry point 0.62 rad past it and an exit point 0.62 rad before it; ring lanes join the points anticlockwise as seen from above (decreasing screen angle, y points down). The exit count in a prompt is the ring lanes on the way that lead to an exit.
- **The car park.** A 70 × 33 m lot behind the north pavement with two rows of 20 bays (2.7 × 5.5 m), half of them taken by parked cars. It is one lane in the graph (`LOT>Q1:0`, from the Q2 gate to the Q1 gate): up the east aisle, west along the north one, south into the target bay and on through the empty bay behind it, west along the south aisle and down to the exit gate, all forward, so the proof never reverses. Its end is a give-way line.
- **Furniture.** Centre lines dashed, solid for the last 15 m before a junction (the one-way street's divider for its last 20 m, where the lane arrows are); stop and give-way lines at controlled mouths; three zebras; generic Vienna-convention signs (speed limits a few metres past the junction that starts the zone, give way, stop, no entry at the wrong end of the one-way street and at the car park's exit gate, one way at the street's start); traffic lights at the crossroads (12 s green, 3 s amber, 1 s all red per phase, two phases, from the test's clock: `light_at`); lay-bys with parked cars on two roads; buildings set back behind the pavements.
- **The asphalt** (`on_road`): road rectangles, junction squares with their rounded corners cut out, the roundabout's ring and flares, the lay-bys and the car park. The kerb check reads it.

### Routes and the examiner

Three routes (`route.ts`): Market (1.2 km), Station (1.3 km) and School (1.4 km), each a start on a road and a list of roads. `build_route` picks the lanes backwards from the end, so on the one-way street the route takes the lane its next turn needs, and joins them through the junction lanes it finds; the result is one polyline with the lane of every sample. Every route has lights, the roundabout, a stop or give-way, the car park mid-route and a pull-up on the last road.

- **Limit zones** (`limits`, read by `limit_at(route, s)` at the rear axle): a lower limit starts at its sign (the sign facing the lane within its first 20 m), a higher one at the junction where the car leaves the lower zone, so out of the car park's gate the road's 50 holds. The HUD's limit sign and the speed check both read them; a lane's own `limit` only says which zone it belongs to.
- **Events** on the line: each junction mouth (its control, the turn made there, the signal it needs, whether it's a real junction rather than a bend or a roundabout entry, the next road) and each roundabout exit (a right signal).
- **Prompts**: the start ("drive on when you're ready…"), every turn at a junction ("At the lights, turn left", "At the end of the road, turn right", "Take the next road on the left"), the roundabout ("take the second exit"), into the car park, out of it (only once the bay is parked), and "Pull up on the right". Each is spoken a few seconds before its junction: when the car is within `max(35 m, 6 s × speed)` of it. Straight on through a junction and the bends get none. A turn's prompt may come before a junction the car goes straight through (Market's car park after B): a signal for the asked-for turn (`prompt.signal`, `prompted(state)`) is no fault there. When that junction has a way in on the turn's side, the prompt waits (`prompt.after`) until the car is through it: on Station and School "Turn left into the car park" comes after Q1, the exit gate, not before it.
- **The route ahead**: with Guides on, a dashed line runs 90 m along the route from the car (`route_span`, cut at exact distances along the route so its ends slide rather than step a sample at a time). Its dash phase is the distance along the route, so the dashes stand still on the road like paint while the car drives over them; phased from the nearest sample instead, they jumped half a metre every half metre driven.
- The bubble shows the last prompt for 9 s; the speech service reads it in the window's voice when Sound is on, newest first. Muting, a new test and closing the game stop it.

### The round

`advance(state, input, dt, spec)`: the car steps, and a parked car or a building stops it and ends the test (a dangerous fault, "The examiner stopped the test"). The car is located on the route near its last place (`locate`, 20 m back to 80 m ahead; anywhere along the car park's stretch while in it). More than 6.5 m off the route for 0.6 s (40 m in the car park) stops the test: "You left the route". Driving into the one-way street the wrong way adds a serious no-entry fault first. Then the checks run, and the examiner speaks if the car is near the next prompt.

- **The bay park.** P with the car over the target bay: inside the lines and within 5° either way round is a park scored by the core's `fit` (Tight Spot's precision); outside or crooked is a serious fault, under 50 % a minor. The exit prompt follows. Driving out of the car park without parking is serious.
- **Pulling up.** Once the last prompt is spoken, P ends the test: more than 0.6 m from the right kerb is a minor, no right signal in the last 12 s a minor. Reaching the end of the route without pulling up is serious.
- **Pass** with at most 15 minors and no serious or dangerous fault (`passes`). A test the examiner stopped has no result.

### The checks

Pure (`rules.ts`): `judge(watch, view)` returns the faults of one step and the watch carried to the next (how long over the limit, whether a fault has been raised for this episode, the signal's history). One fault per episode: it re-arms once the driver is back within the rule. Lines and junction events are judged at the front bumper (`at.s` plus the body's front), so a car that crosses on amber isn't faulted when its rear crosses on red.

| Check | Fault | Rule |
|-------|-------|------|
| Speed | minor, serious | over the zone's limit + max(1 m/s, 10 %) for 1 s; serious over 1.3 × limit + 1 m/s for 0.5 s; each burst gets its own second (the clock resets back within the tolerance); re-arms under the limit |
| Road markings | minor | the body crosses a solid line |
| Traffic lights | serious | the car crosses the stop line on red |
| Stop sign | serious | slowest speed in the last 6 m before the line over 0.3 m/s |
| Give way | minor | slowest speed in the last 6 m over 4.5 m/s |
| Signals | minor | at a junction turn: the turning side shown for less than 0.7 s in the last 6 s (so a stalk that cancelled on the way in still counts); the other side shown is "signalled left, turned right"; a signal on going straight through a junction, unless it's for the turn the examiner has asked for; no right signal leaving the roundabout; none before pulling up |
| Lane choice | minor | at the one-way street's stop line, nearer the other lane's middle than the route's |
| Kerb | minor, serious | a body corner off the asphalt; serious with the body's middle off it |
| Lane position | minor, serious | on a road lane, the middle more than 1.1 m from any lane of the car's direction for 2 s; serious past the centre line for 0.8 s; not in junctions, the car park or once asked to pull up |
| Hesitation | minor | stopped for 4 s with nothing to wait for (6 s with the front within 15 m of a stop or give-way line; within 15 m of lights on red or amber doesn't count, so a car may queue two lengths back, and the wait starts at green; not before moving off, in the car park or pulling up) |
| Bay park, pulling up, contact, no entry | as above | |

The sheet (`sheet`) groups the faults by kind and severity, worst first, with each kind's notes and a line on why it matters (`FAULT_INFO`).

### Saved data and screen

`test.json` in the app data folder: `{ best }`, the best result per route, `{ passed, minors, serious, dangerous, ms }`: a pass beats a fail, then fewer serious and dangerous, fewer minors, the quicker drive (`better`). `app.tight-spot.test.route` keeps the last route, `app.tight-spot.test.landscape-hint` that the one-time phone hint was seen. No daily yet.

The camera follows the car with 1.1 s of look-ahead and widens from the hatchback's parking span to 62 m at top speed (only with Zoom with speed on, as in Hot Lap); before the start and on the result card it frames the car at 48 m. The HUD: the gear strip and speed, the speed limit sign of the zone the car is in, the signal arrows (fine pointer; touch has the buttons), the minors, serious faults and the clock, a chip for each new fault for 2.6 s, the examiner's bubble. An arrow at the board's edge points to the bay from 80 m before the car park. The start card names the route, its length and the best; the result card says Pass, Fail or Test stopped, the counts, the bay's precision, and the sheet (scrolls). Achievements: `test-pass`, `test-clean` (no faults), `test-routes` (every route passed, goal 3), `test-bay` (a Perfect bay).

### Tests

## Limits

- Kinematic only: no slip, so speed changes nothing about the path. Fine at parking speed; drift is the planned dynamic model.
- Playtested from Playwright on the owner's dev server, desktop and a 390 × 844 touch emulation; not on a real phone or with a real wheel feel review.
- The planner proves a path with 5 cm of clearance, not that a person finds it easy; difficulty ranges are tuned by eye.
- The open lot is fixed at 3 × 3 and only uses four kinds of lot. Kerb bays are only on its east–west roads, one lay-by a side per arm, and the trailer gets none.
- Precision isn't comparable across kinds: it's a share of each bay's room, and the rooms differ.
- The bus stop has no tapered lay-by: a 32 m lot has no room for one with cars at both ends.
- A district grows only along its own row, so one with many games stretches the court in one direction: a big Town makes a long, thin town.
- Mow Day's coverable lawn is a coarse lattice's reach, not every cell a driver could get to: a careful driver can cut a little more than counts, never less than the 90 % needed. The overlap and the par are formulas tuned on an ideal up-and-down run, not on players.
- The Warehouse's par time takes the planner one to six seconds in a worker; the card shows "…" meanwhile. A job finished first is recorded straight away and gets its time star when the par arrives; a second job finished before the first's par arrives replaces the wait, so the first never gets that star.
- Parking's dailies from before the per-game split stay under `tight-spot` in `plays.json`; Game Hub no longer shows them.
- The tractor, the tractor with two trailers and the baggage train have no spot yet. A chain doesn't collide with itself: a body folding into the one ahead shows overlapping until the hinge's limit ends the round. The coach only takes the depot (some bus-stop seeds failed).
- The Driving Test has no moving traffic or pedestrians yet: give way means slow down, a zebra is paint. A wrong turn stops the test rather than re-routing. The town is one fixed map, and the bay park is forward only in the proof (a player may reverse in).
- A contact in the yard stops the vehicle dead; nothing is pushed, cones included.
- Valet Rush's walks are a jump of the clock by straight-line distance, through lots and kerbs; nobody is drawn walking. Owners' cars come back without the steering angle they were parked with. With both north-road lanes beside a lot gate, the one coach bay sits beside the east gate and a bus parked there shuts that lot until it's fetched. A third bus while two are parked has nowhere to go; the schedule spaces buses eight arrivals apart, so it needs two unfetched.
- A skid steer is kinematic like everything else: its sides scrub sideways only as far as the wider `track` stands in for, and it turns the same on any ground. The ladder truck is all-wheel steer, not a tiller truck with a second driver; the telehandler has no two-wheel mode. A skid steer, a frame steer or a vehicle crabbing tows nothing: `step` and `travel` move a chain only behind an Ackermann arc, and no such vehicle has one.
